# DIVISION H: The Psychology Layer (attention, curiosity, saves, sends, follows)

Compiled 2026-07-10 by the Division H head from four scout sweeps (h1 scroll-stopping science, h2 curiosity-gap and retention, h3 save/share/send psychology, h4 follow conversion) plus two head-level verification searches. Recency-weighted to Apr-Jul 2026. Peer-reviewed academic work is TIER 1 (citable as fact). Creator/marketing-blog benchmarks are TIER 2 (directional, private data, treat numbers as ranges). Every figure below appears in a page a scout fetched or a search result the head saw. Unverified numbers are labeled.

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The redesign toward posters is not an aesthetic preference. It is what the attention and save/send psychology literally rewards, and Carter's own 90-day data (artifact decks 47k-65k views / 2,700-6,600 saves vs text-template decks 10k-18k views / 100-570 saves) is a clean in-niche replication of it. The brain extracts an image's gist in 13 ms (Potter, MIT) before reading starts, so slide 1 must broadcast ONE concept pre-attentively at the optical CENTER (Tatler central fixation bias), never a top banner. The single biggest untapped lever in the entire research set is MOTION: dynamic beat static at F=245.896 (MDPI, Dec 2025), the largest effect size found, and Carter's pipeline can ship it two native ways (a video first slide, or a swipe-driven flipbook deck) without breaking carousel treatment. Saves and sends run on DIFFERENT psychology (save = future utility for the self; send = endorsement/identity for a recipient), and sends-per-reach is the 2026 king metric Carter's save-heavy decks leave on the table. The cover slide's job is to open ONE curiosity gap, and the strongest primary finding says the gap must be a PARTIAL reveal: over-concreteness (the cover answers itself) costs about twice what over-vagueness does (Nature meta-analysis of 8,977 headlines). The 700-vs-1 follow spread is decided upstream by reach (saves + sends + completion), not by the last slide.

## THE FINDINGS THAT MATTER (ranked, each with source and tier)

### 1. The gist lands in 13 ms, so slide 1 must be understood without reading. TIER 1.
Potter, Wyble, Hagmann, McCourt, "Detecting meaning in RSVP at 13 ms per picture," Attention Perception & Psychophysics, 2014. Subjects identified a named concept in images shown as briefly as 13 ms via feedforward processing, no time for a deliberate read. On a feed the viewer gets the concept before reading begins, so a slide that must be READ to be understood has already lost. This is the hard science under "poster not newspaper."
Source: news.mit.edu/2014/in-the-blink-of-an-eye-0116 ; link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13414-013-0605-z

### 2. Motion is the biggest lever we are not using, and it ships two native ways. TIER 1 effect + TIER 2 mechanics.
The MDPI Applied Sciences eye-tracking study (60 participants, published 2025-12-27, Vol 16 Issue 1 Art 291, "An Eye-Tracking Study of Attention Capture Efficiency in Commercial Poster Design," head-verified as a real publication) found dynamic posters crushed static for total fixation duration and count at F=245.896, p<0.001, the LARGEST effect in the whole set. Color also beat black-and-white at F=5.067, p=0.028.
Head-added, TIER 2, how to ship it in Carter's pipeline without breaking carousel treatment:
- A VIDEO first slide. Instagram carousels support mixed media; video slides run up to 60 seconds at 1080x1350 (multiple 2026 spec sources: contentdrips, storrito, carouselmaker). shotpls renders HTML, so a slide can be exported as a short MP4 loop (a number counting, a bar filling, a term glitching in).
- A "Hold and Scroll" FLIPBOOK deck. A sequence of near-identical static 1080x1350 slides that Instagram plays as flipbook animation when the viewer holds the dots and swipes fast (yoursocial.team, pub 2026-10 context, describes the exact 6-step build at 1080x1350). This is a pure static carousel, no video, so zero format-treatment risk, and it is 100% in-pipeline for shotpls.
CONTRADICTION TO FLAG: the MDPI study used commercial posters viewed on a screen, not a scrolling mobile carousel. Direction (motion beats static) is very likely to transfer but no eye-tracking study tests an animated slide INSIDE a carousel specifically. Treat as strong-directional, then measure on our own decks.
Source: mdpi.com/2076-3417/16/1/291 ; contentdrips.com/blog/2026/05/instagram-carousel-size-format ; yoursocial.team blog "Hold and Scroll"

### 3. First fixation is the CENTER, not the top-left. TIER 1.
Tatler, "The central fixation bias in scene viewing," Journal of Vision, 2007 (PubMed 18217799). A suddenly-appearing image pulls the first ~1-2 seconds of gaze to the CENTER, strongest at onset, independent of where features sit; it decays only after 1000-2000 ms. A feed slide is a sudden-onset image. The F-pattern / top-left (Nielsen/Pernice) applies only AFTER a viewer commits to reading text. Rule: anchor the single hero element at or just above optical center, never in a top banner.
Source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18217799 ; jov.arvojournals.org/article.aspx?articleid=2661519

### 4. The cover-slide curiosity gap is CURVILINEAR; over-concreteness is the dominant failure. TIER 1. (Highest-value curiosity finding.)
Aubin Le Quere & Matias, "When curiosity gaps backfire," Scientific Reports (Nature), 2025-01-06. Meta-analysis of 8,977 Upworthy headline experiments. The concreteness-to-clickthrough curve is U-shaped: too vague, adding specificity lifts clicks about +5.5%; too concrete, more specificity CUTS clicks about -9.9%. 50.9% of tested headlines were over-concrete vs only 8.7% too vague. So the cover must be a PARTIAL reveal (specific enough that the payoff feels real, exactly one thing withheld), and because over-concreteness costs roughly 2x over-vagueness, if you must err, err slightly vague. Kills the "answer your own question on the cover" failure that gives permission to leave.
Source: nature.com/articles/s41598-024-81575-9 via pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11704130

### 5. Faces get automatic capture; tense faces fastest; eyeline cues attention. TIER 1.
PMC eye-tracking study (35 users, Tobii 1200 Hz, simulated Instagram, 2025): human and promotional elements drew the most gaze. Threat expressions (angry/fearful) are detected at ~90 ms vs ~500 ms for neutral, via the amygdala before conscious awareness. Gaze cueing: a model's eyeline drags attention to whatever it points at. Rule when a face is present: a high-tension expression aimed at the headline word, never a stock grin at camera.
Source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12641903 ; jneurosci.org/content/43/8/1405 ; researchgate.net/publication/323283511

### 6. Simplicity wins on TWO independent axes: fluency (liking) and clutter (speed/accuracy). TIER 1.
Reber, Schwarz, Winkielman, "Processing Fluency and Aesthetic Pleasure," 2004: higher processing fluency directly raises liking (figure-ground contrast, symmetry, figural goodness). Separately, the clutter literature (Moacdieh & Sarter; Rosenholtz lineage) shows clutter degrades search, raising fixation count, duration, scan-path length, and error rate. Fewer reading zones simultaneously raises PREFERENCE and SPEED. This is the double-justified spine under "poster not newspaper," and it matches Carter's 47k-vs-14k data exactly.
Source: pages.ucsd.edu/~pwinkiel/reber-schwarz-winkielman-beauty-PSPR-2004.pdf ; sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0003687019300924

### 7. SAVE and SEND are different psychology, rewarded differently. Sends-per-reach is the 2026 king metric. TIER 1 mechanism / TIER 2 platform.
Save = private future-utility bookmark for the self (checklists, frameworks, tool lists, salary tables, roadmaps, before/afters). Send = a recommendation that transfers trust or signals identity about the sender. Mosseri (stated Jan 2025, restated through 2026, via Buffer): the top signals are watch time, sends-per-reach, and likes-per-reach, and sends-per-reach is the most powerful signal for reaching NEW audiences (a save is one selfish bookmark; a DM send seeds a new viewer and reads as the strongest endorsement). ">3% sends-per-reach" is cited as the threshold for heavy non-follower distribution (buffer.com; the exact 3-5x-weight multiplier is TIER 2 aggregator paraphrase). This is the metric Carter's save-heavy artifact decks currently under-fire.
Source: buffer.com/resources/instagram-algorithms ; influencermarketinghub.com/instagram-sends-per-reach-playbook ; socialmon.ai 2026-07-02

### 8. High-arousal emotion is the transmission fuel; low-arousal suppresses sends. TIER 1.
Berger & Milkman, "What Makes Online Content Viral?," JMR 2012, plus the 2025 Frontiers in Psychology systematic review (66 studies, PMC12174407). Awe, righteous anger, and anxiety drive sharing; low-arousal sadness/contentment suppress it. Anger was the single strongest predictor of the NYT most-emailed list; awe the strongest positive. Cyber registers that travel: awe-tools ("maps a whole network in 4 seconds"), righteous anger ("companies STILL store passwords in plaintext in 2026"), anxiety ("the 5 ways you're already exposed"). Neutral/calm content saves but does not send.
Source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12174407 ; jonahberger.com (numbers via secondary sources)

### 9. The dominant save motive is FUTURE UTILITY, and saving feels like completion. TIER 2 + adjacent TIER 1.
socialmon (2026-07-02), verbatim: "People do not save entertainment, they save utility." Reinforced by three mechanisms: the save is a gift to the future self (Stylist UK psychologist piece; Hershfield future-self research), it is private zero-social-cost consumption (saves decouple from likes), and the Collector's Fallacy means saving FEELS like completion so saves are cheap to give (under ~20% of bookmarks are ever revisited). Make the deck LOOK like a complete, findable, worth-hoarding artifact and the save fires. Name the concrete future moment in the save-CTA ("save this the night before your Security+ exam," "keep this for your next salary negotiation") rather than a generic "save this."
Source: socialmon.ai 2026-07-02 ; stylist.co.uk ; zettelkasten.de/posts/collectors-fallacy

### 10. Completion rate is a hidden reach/follow engine via the re-serve mechanic. TIER 2.
marketingagent.blog (2026-01-03): when a viewer does not finish, Instagram treats unseen slides as "new content" and re-serves the deck later from a different slide, giving one post multiple distribution passes. Longer decks with strong LATER slides create more re-serve entry points. Cited (uncorroborated): 7-10 slides generate ~3.5x more dwell than single images and ~22% more non-follower reach; forcing 3+ swipes before the payoff tied to up to 40% more Explore placement. Completion is a reach multiplier, not vanity. CONTRADICTS the naive "keep it short" advice; the resolution is "long enough to create re-serve entry points, with genuine per-slide payoff."
Source: marketingagent.blog/2026/01/03

### 11. Follows are decided upstream (reach), then closed by a series-shaped profile, not the last slide. TIER 2.
Two-stage funnel: the POST earns the profile visit (via saves/sends/completion driving non-follower reach), the PROFILE earns the follow. Published benchmark: profile-visit-to-follow averages ~13.5% monthly, 20-30% elite (flick.social). So ~700 follows needs roughly 3,000-5,000 profile visits. The strongest DELIBERATE follow lever is a named, numbered, visually-consistent SERIES recognizable in under 2 seconds (creatorflow, truefuturemedia): people follow to not miss the next installment (macro open-loop). "Random topics create random followers." The final slide is a trigger with ONE ask, value-first; the profile (pinned trio About You / Proof / Offer, routing-layer bio) is the close and Carter's likely biggest leak.
Source: flick.social/learn/instagram-analytics-benchmarks ; creatorflow.so/blog/instagram-content-series-strategy ; avramify.com pinned-posts ; truefuturemedia bio-optimization-2026

### 12. Zeigarnik open loops drive RESUMPTION, not memory. TIER 1 caveat.
A 2025 systematic review/meta-analysis found NO reliable memory advantage for unfinished tasks but a real tendency to RESUME them. Better framing for carousels: the pull to CLOSE the loop is the swipe drive. Layer one macro-loop (opens on cover, closes on the payoff slide, and for a series stays open into the next installment) with a chain of micro-loops (each slide teases the next). Stop citing the "90% better recall" number as settled. Curiosity fires the moment a gap is felt; fMRI shows the left caudate (dopamine) responds when a gap opens (Loewenstein info-gap theory).
Source: 2025 Zeigarnik meta-analysis (via snippet + Wikipedia framing) ; cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/golman

### 13. Odd counts double as a progress-bar-shaped curiosity gap. TIER 1 chunking / TIER 2 lift.
Odd-numbered titles measured ~+21% clicks vs an identical even one (TIER 2). Three mechanisms: chunking in 3s/5s aids retention (TIER 1), odd reads as authentic not padded, and a visible count ("3 / 7") is a quantified, closable gap that doubles as a progress bar (ties to the finite-boundary "wait until slide 7" mechanic). Prefer 5/7/9 counts, rendered as a thin mono progress element, never a billboard.
Source: talkedaboutmarketing / blogherald aggregation ; chunking basis is TIER 1 working-memory literature

## CONTRADICTIONS FLAGGED (both sources named)

- COVER-SLIDE WEIGHT: "slide 1 carries ~60% of swipe-through" (postwaffle / reelbase, TIER 2) vs "~80% of the weight" (postnitro 2025-12-15; postworthy; truefuturemedia, TIER 2). Honest read: the cover dominates the outcome, somewhere 60-80%. Do not quote a single figure.
- DECK LENGTH: "keep it short / minimal" (poster thesis, Sagum negative-space) vs "7-13 slides for re-serve reach and dwell" (marketingagent, finding 10). Resolution: each SLIDE stays poster-minimal (one focal point), but the DECK runs long enough (7-9) to create re-serve entry points and completion dwell. Minimal per slide, not minimal in count.
- "NATIVE UN-DESIGNED SCREENSHOT LOOK" +2.3x engagement (Sagum, single-source, TIER 2) CONTRADICTS Carter's premium brutalist artifact identity and his own winning data. Reject for this brand; it is niche-dependent.
- MOTION TRANSFER: MDPI dynamic-vs-static (F=245.896) is on screen-viewed POSTERS, not a mobile carousel. Direction transfers by strong inference, not by a carousel-specific study. Measure on our decks before treating as fact.
- MIXED-MEDIA ENGAGEMENT DECIMALS ("2.33% vs 1.80%") surfaced only in a search AI summary and did NOT appear on the source pages the head fetched. Do NOT cite that decimal as fact; the DIRECTION (a short clip inside a carousel spikes dwell) is echoed on truefuturemedia ("a few short clips inside the carousel can spike dwell time").

## DIRECTIVES FOR THE REBUILD (numbered, concrete)

1. HARD RULE for slide 1: one focal point, optically CENTERED, legible as a 13 ms gist with zero reading. One giant number, one artifact image (badge/book/tool/screenshot), or one 3-5 word claim. If a human must read it to get it, the gist pass failed. Backed by Potter + Tatler.

2. BUILD an animated first-slide path as a first-class pipeline feature. Two variants from the existing shotpls/headless-Chrome rig: (a) export slide 1 as a short MP4 loop (video slide, up to 60s at 1080x1350), and (b) a "Hold and Scroll" flipbook deck of near-identical static slides. Motion is the largest documented effect (F=245.896). The flipbook is zero-risk (pure static carousel) and fully in-pipeline; prototype it first.

3. CONCRETENESS-SWEET-SPOT checklist item in make-carousel: the cover states the topic AND withholds the specific payoff. If a viewer could get the value without swiping, it is too concrete (the dominant failure, ~2x costlier than vagueness). Err slightly vague if forced. Nature 8,977-headline meta-analysis.

4. Slide 1 is the highest-contrast, most-saturated slide of the deck, full-bleed, no logo-first runway, hook rendered first. Branding is a small lockup, never the hook. Front-load into the first 1-3 seconds.

5. Design the deck save-worthy AT THE CORE (a keepable reference artifact: cheat sheet, tool list, salary map, roadmap, before/after) AND inject exactly ONE engineered SEND moment: high-arousal register (awe / righteous anger / anxiety) plus a named-recipient ask ("send this to a friend trying to break into cyber"). Numeric quotas ("tag 10 friends") are Meta engagement-bait and BANNED. Saves and sends come from different slides.

6. Name the future moment of need in the final-slide save-CTA ("save this the night before your Security+ exam," "keep this for your next salary negotiation"), never a generic "save this."

7. Layer ONE macro-loop (opens on cover, closes on the payoff slide) with a chain of micro-loops (each slide teases the next). Frame the driver as RESUMPTION PULL, not memory. Never open a loop you do not close; a flat reveal burns trust across future decks.

8. Prefer ODD counts (5/7/9) and show a thin mono "3 / 7" progress marker: a quantified, closable curiosity gap plus a chunking aid. Fits the editorial/brutalist system, not a billboard element.

9. Run each SLIDE poster-minimal (one focal point, generous negative space) but the DECK at 7-9 slides so the re-serve mechanic and completion dwell fire. Minimal per slide, not minimal in count.

10. Ship decks as a NAMED, NUMBERED SERIES with 3-5 locked templates, recognizable in under 2 seconds ("[Series] 03 of 09"). This is the strongest deliberate follow lever; people follow to close the macro-loop. Make it a required field in make-carousel.

11. Final slide = ONE ask, value delivered on the penultimate slide first. Follow-goal decks: "Follow so you don't miss [Series] 04," tied to the narrow topic. Lead-goal decks: a comment-keyword DM ask. Add a small persistent "follow for more" footer (Double CTA). Never stack CTAs.

12. Fix the PROFILE (the follow close, likely the biggest leak): pin the strategic trio (About You / Proof / Offer), make the bio a routing layer (keyword + brand in Name field, one CTA), keep the artifact grid recognizable through the poster redesign. Track saves-per-reach AND sends-per-reach AND profile-visit-to-follow as SEPARATE KPIs, plus episode-2 retention above 30% as the series-health signal.

## OPEN QUESTIONS

1. No eye-tracking study tests an animated slide INSIDE a scrolling mobile carousel. The motion effect (F=245.896) is inferred to transfer from screen-viewed posters. Only our own A/B (animated vs static slide 1) can confirm it for this format.

2. Per-slide swipe/completion data is owner-PRIVATE and unscrapable (confirmed via sociavault), so no competitor per-slide A/B curve exists in the open. Instagram is rolling out native per-slide Insights in 2026. When Carter's account gets it, we can generate PROPRIETARY per-slide + cover-concreteness A/B data the whole niche lacks. Highest-leverage build item.

3. The mixed-media engagement decimals (2.33% vs 1.80%) could not be verified on any fetched source page. Whether a video first slide out-reaches a static one, and whether music-on-a-video-slide pushing the deck toward the Reels feed helps or hurts SAVE rate, is untested for this brand. A/B it.

4. Whether a video/flipbook slide 1 changes Instagram's format treatment or grid crop for Carter's account specifically is spec-plausible (first slide sets aspect ratio for all slides, confirmed) but unverified in practice. Test on a throwaway before a flagship deck.

5. No cybersecurity-niche-specific published save/send/follow dataset exists. The "cheat sheets save, insider tools send" split is synthesis of the general psychology plus Carter's own 90-day artifact-vs-template numbers, not an external niche study.

6. No study isolates a specific save RATE to a specific slide-1 design variable, and no controlled test isolates the marginal follow contribution of the last slide alone. Both causal chains are assembled from separate literatures plus Carter's own spread, not one integrated experiment.

7. Several 2026 macro-stats (attention-span figures, the "leaked 2024 Meta 3-5x report," the "identity content shares 3.4x more" claim, Sagum's 3.7x/34%/28% multipliers) are single-source or untraceable to a primary document. Direction only; do not quote as fact.
