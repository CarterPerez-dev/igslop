## FINDINGS

The core distinction this mission has to nail: SAVES and SENDS are driven by DIFFERENT psychology and rewarded DIFFERENTLY by the 2026 algorithm. Save = "this is useful to FUTURE ME" (private, selfish, utility). Send = "this says something about ME to YOU, or solves YOUR problem" (social, endorsement, relational). Design for one does not automatically get you the other. Below, each of the four psychology layers, then the exact moves that trigger each.

---

### LAYER 1: ATTENTION and CURIOSITY (getting the swipe at all)

**1.1 Loewenstein's information-gap theory is the engine (BACKGROUND, 1994, still the canonical model).**
George Loewenstein: curiosity is a feeling of DEPRIVATION that arises when a person becomes consciously aware of a gap between what they know and what they want to know. The gap creates cognitive tension (a "cognitive itch") that the brain is motivated to close. Neuro layer: curiosity stimulates the brain's reward/dopamine system, so closing the gap is literally rewarding. Source: Loewenstein, "The Psychology of Curiosity" (1994); CMU Golman/Loewenstein "Curiosity, Information Gaps, and the Utility of Knowledge" PDF (cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/golman). Recent restatement: Medium/Ishwarya Pandian "Information Gap Theory" (Nov 2025); Journal of Cognition "Knowledge Gap Illustrations Spark Curiosity" (journalofcognition.org/articles/10.5334/joc.501).

Implication for a carousel: the cover slide's ONLY job is to open a gap the viewer needs closed. Not to inform. To create a specific, itch-inducing absence. A poster that states one bold thing and withholds the resolution outperforms a newspaper slide that answers itself.

**1.2 The 50-millisecond scroll-stop (2025-2026, creator-source, directional).**
carouselli / octospark 2025-2026 guides: strong visual pattern-interrupts (high-contrast color, expressive faces, striking single graphic) stop the scroll "in under 50 milliseconds," and the hook headline should be 5-8 words creating a curiosity gap, with specific numbers and outcomes "outperforming generic descriptions significantly." Confidence: the 50ms figure is a repeated creator claim, not a cited lab number, treat as directional. The DIRECTION (one high-contrast focal element, specific number, short headline) is consistent across every 2026 source.

**1.3 Open loops carry attention slide-to-slide (2026, socialmon 2026-07-02).**
socialmon "55 viral carousel ideas" (2026-07-02): the open-loop structure is "hold strongest points for final slides; end most slides on partial thoughts that the next slide completes." This is Loewenstein applied per-slide: every slide re-opens a small gap so the swipe is compelled, not optional. Target depth 6-10 slides. This is directly relevant to the "poster not newspaper" thesis: a poster slide that resolves itself gives the viewer permission to leave; a poster slide that dangles pulls the swipe.

### LAYER 2: SAVES (future utility, identity, self-compassion)

**2.1 Two save archetypes: "Super-Liked" vs "Finish-Later" (psychologist-sourced, BACKGROUND but clarifying).**
Stylist UK, "What do Instagram Saves say about you? A psychologist explains" (stylist.co.uk/life/save-instagram-photo-video-meaning): two distinct save reasons. "Super-Liked" = casually saved because it is "too good to just deserve a like," may or may not revisit. "Finish-Later" = seriously saved because it is valuable/educational and too long to finish in the moment, genuine intent to return. Design consequence: a carousel wants to trigger EITHER, but the Finish-Later save is the one tied to reference/utility content and is the higher-intent, algorithm-loved one.

**2.2 The dominant save motive is FUTURE UTILITY. "People do not save entertainment, they save utility."**
socialmon (2026-07-02), verbatim: "People do not save entertainment, they save utility." The high-save content types they list: checklists, frameworks, decision trees, glossaries, starter kits, templates, before/after proof, month-by-month progress. Corroborated by Socialinsider 2025-2026 benchmark data: education and personal-brand accounts "consistently top the engagement charts because their carousel content carries high perceived value. People save educational carousels to refer back to later." (socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/instagram-engagement-report). This is the single most important finding for a cybersecurity creator: cheat sheets, tool lists, command references, salary tables, roadmaps ARE the save-maximizing archetype. Carter's own 90-day data (image-artifact decks 2,700-6,600 saves vs text-template decks 100-570 saves) is this exact effect: the badge/book decks read as reference artifacts worth keeping.

**2.3 The save is an act of self-compassion / a gift to the future self (psychologist-sourced).**
Stylist UK (same source): the act of saving is "a form of self-compassion, a gesture of care for your future self who might need that exact inspiration." This connects to the intertemporal-self literature: people who feel connected to a vividly imagined future self are more motivated to act for that self (Hershfield et al., future-self / age-progression saving studies, PMC3949005). Copy consequence: framing that names the future moment of need ("save this for the next time you're staring at a blank terminal / studying for the exam / negotiating salary") makes the future-self benefit concrete and raises save intent.

**2.4 Saving is private, low-social-risk consumption.**
Stylist UK / Blossom Marketing: saves let people "consume Instagram without openly expressing that they 'like' something to the entire internet," curating thoughts "behind closed doors." Unlike a like/comment (public), a save has zero social cost. Consequence: content that is embarrassing-to-publicly-endorse but privately valuable (beginner-level "how do I even start," "dumb question" content) can save well even when it does not get public likes. Saves and likes decouple.

**2.5 The Collector's Fallacy: saving FEELS like learning, which is exactly why people do it so freely (BACKGROUND, but explains save volume).**
Coined by Christian Tietze (zettelkasten.de/posts/collectors-fallacy). "The brain registers the act of saving as a form of completion" and it "quells the anxiety that we'll never find this again." Data cited across sources: fewer than ~20% of bookmarked content is ever revisited; the average knowledge worker saves 3-5 articles/day and reads under 30% of them (sinapsus.com/blog/the-collectors-fallacy; mindmastersdaily.com). Read this cynically-but-usefully: saves are CHEAP for the user to give because saving relieves anxiety instantly and requires no follow-through. That is GOOD for a creator whose goal is the save signal. The move is to make the artifact LOOK like a complete, findable, worth-hoarding reference so the anxiety-relief save fires. You are not obligated to make them actually study it.

**2.6 Save benchmarks and the algorithmic payoff (2025-2026, creator-source, directional).**
Socialinsider 2025 study (15M posts, 417,130 pages, Oct 2025-Mar 2026 window per one 2026 restatement): carousels average the highest engagement of all formats (~1.92% vs 1.74% Reels vs 1.22% single image in the 2025 cut) and carousels get "3x more saves than Reels." Save rate benchmarks by size (creator-source, carouselli / creatorflow): 5k-100k accounts, 3-6% engagement is solid, 6-10%+ strong; education/personal-brand carousels frequently hit 8-12% on best posts. The payoff: a high save rate signals "long-term utility" to the algorithm, which extends distribution to "days rather than hours" via Explore/recommendations. Confidence: format-comparison direction is well-supported; exact decimals are aggregator numbers, treat as directional.

### LAYER 3: SENDS (relational value, self-presentation, the 2026 king metric)

**3.1 Sends-per-reach is the single most important 2026 ranking signal, and it is DIFFERENT from saves.**
Mosseri (stated Jan 2025, restated through 2026): the top ranking signals are watch time, sends-per-reach (DM shares), and likes-per-reach, and sends-per-reach is "the most powerful signal for reaching new audiences." Widely reported: sends carry roughly 3-5x more weight than likes when deciding whether to push to non-followers (creatorflow.so/blog/instagram-algorithm-2026; dataslayer.ai). Mosseri's own instruction: "think about creating something people want to send to a friend." Confidence: the ranking-priority statement is genuinely Mosseri-sourced and repeated everywhere; the exact "3-5x" multiplier is aggregator paraphrase.

Why sends beat saves algorithmically (InfluencerMarketingHub sends-per-reach playbook, 2026-03-03): a save is a PERSONAL bookmark (selfish, one person). A DM send is a RECOMMENDATION/ENDORSEMENT that transfers trust to a second person and communicates "intent, trust, and emotional resonance." Private sharing is "more deliberate" than a passive like, so IG treats it as the strongest possible endorsement. Sends also directly seed a NEW viewer, which saves do not.

**3.2 The two send motives: (a) it says something about ME, or (b) it solves YOUR problem.**
This is where classic virality science and 2026 platform mechanics converge.

Motive (a): SELF-PRESENTATION / SOCIAL CURRENCY. Jonah Berger's STEPPS (Contagious, BACKGROUND): "the better something makes us look, the more likely we are to share." People forward content that makes them appear knowledgeable, insightful, ahead-of-the-curve, or aligned with the right values. The 2025 Frontiers systematic review (66 studies, 2020-2024, Cai/Kamarudin/Nujaimi, Frontiers in Psychology 2025-06-04) confirms self-presentation and impression-management as distinct sharing frameworks: "people motivated by self-image goals are more likely to share self-promoting content." For a cyber creator: a slick "here are the 12 tools real pentesters actually use" deck lets the sender forward it as PROOF they know the space. They are borrowing your authority to build their own.

Motive (b): PRACTICAL VALUE FOR A SPECIFIC RECIPIENT. Berger & Milkman, "What Makes Online Content Viral?" (Journal of Marketing Research, 2012, BACKGROUND, full NYT most-emailed corpus over 3 months): practically useful, surprising, and interesting content is more viral; "news you can use" gets shared to help others and generate reciprocity. The 2026 send-ask copy exploits this by naming the recipient: "send this to a friend who's trying to break into cyber," "DM this to the person who keeps asking how you got certified."

**3.3 High-arousal EMOTION is the transmission fuel (BACKGROUND, Berger & Milkman 2012, the most-cited finding).**
The mechanism behind sends is physiological AROUSAL. High-arousal emotions (awe, anger, anxiety, excitement) increase sharing; low-arousal emotions (sadness, contentment) decrease it. Anger was the single most powerful predictor of making the NYT most-emailed list; awe was the strongest positive driver. Corroborated by the 2025 Frontiers review: anger and anxiety significantly shape sharing willingness, with people feeling both simultaneously showing the highest sharing likelihood. Consequence for cyber content: awe ("this one tool maps an entire network in 4 seconds"), righteous anger ("companies are still storing passwords in plaintext in 2026"), and anxiety ("the 5 ways your accounts are already exposed") are the emotionally shareable registers. Sad/calm content does not travel.

**3.4 The 8 sharing motives (NFX / James Currier, 2021-02, BACKGROUND, the best practical taxonomy).**
nfx.com/post/why-people-share. Eight clusters, each with a design implication. The ones most relevant to a cyber-authority creator:
- STATUS ("in-the-know," prestige, scarcity): people share to associate with high-status/exclusive knowledge. Move: "insider" framing, tools/knowledge that signal you are ahead.
- IDENTITY PROJECTION (tribalism, vindication): people define themselves by what they oppose; sharing signals tribe. Move: give the audience "a point of view" to signal with. WallStreetBets lingo is cited. Cyber has strong tribal in-group language (blue team vs red team, "it's always DNS," "trust no one / zero trust").
- BEING HELPFUL (utility, "better/cheaper"): sharing useful things makes the sharer feel competent and nurturing. Currier's copy note: "give your users language to express utility clearly." Move: make the value one-line-articulable so the sender can say what it is in the DM.
- VALIDATION ("smart," positive): shareable outputs that affirm the sharer's identity (personality-test results are the archetype). Move: "which SOC analyst are you," "rate your setup," typing content.
- NOVELTY (the new, entertainment): Wundt-curve sweet spot of familiar-plus-new. Move: a genuinely new tool/technique the audience has not seen.
Currier's meta-point: minimize FRICTION and give users the LANGUAGE to describe what they are sharing. A carousel that is instantly summarizable ("it's the 40 free cyber tools list") sends more than one that requires explanation.

**3.5 Identity-confirming content shares ~3.4x more than merely-informative content (single-source, treat with caution).**
A claim surfaced in search attributed to "the University of Edinburgh's Centre for Digital Media Analysis": content aligned with identity confirmation sees sharing rates "up to 3.4 times higher" than content that merely informs. GAP/CAVEAT: I could not independently verify this institution or a primary paper; it appeared inside a marketing explainer (internetmarketinglife.com), not a locatable study. Use the DIRECTION (identity-reinforcing beats neutral-informative), do NOT cite the 3.4x number as fact.

**3.6 The 2026 send-ask copy rules and named examples (InfluencerMarketingHub, 2026-03-03).**
The playbook's Hook-Body-Send pattern: hook (problem/surprise stat), body (deliver the utility), send-ask that NAMES A RECIPIENT rather than a numeric quota. Verified copy examples from the article:
- "Send this to your team before your next shoot"
- "DM this to a friend who always forgets captions"
- "Know someone with dry skin? Send this"
- "If you know someone launching a podcast, send this checklist"
BANNED by Meta engagement-bait policy (do NOT use): "Share this with 5 friends to unlock part 2," "Tag ten friends." (Confirmed across InfluencerMarketingHub and multiple 2026 algorithm guides.) Named creators/formats the article credits with high DM velocity: Kelsey Kotzur (@kelseykotzur, utility outfit formulas labeled by use case, "coffee run/desk day"); Overheard LA (@overheardla, single-line screenshot memes, Doing Things Media literally measures "DM shares per view" not likes); Dr. Layne Norton (@biolayne, myth-busting posts forwarded "to prove a point," the social-currency motive in action); Katie Jane Hughes (@katiejanehughes, "Don't Do This, Here's Why" format). Format families with natural send velocity: mini-infographics/cheatsheets ("shared as resources or proof points"), relatable one-line/screenshot content ("this is so my friend"), micro-tutorials, and utility carousels.

**3.7 The relatable/meme axis sends; the utility axis saves. They are different content.**
socialmon (2026-07-02) makes the split explicit: reference carousels (checklists, frameworks, templates, before/after) earn the MOST SAVES; relatable carousels ("which one are you," "POV: it's Monday," "tag the friend who does this," niche in-jokes) earn the MOST SHARES; question/hot-take carousels earn the most COMMENTS. This is the operational crux: you cannot maximize saves and sends with the SAME slide. A save-optimized reference deck and a send-optimized relatable/hot-take deck are different products. Best decks stack a save-worthy core with one send-worthy relatable or hot-take moment.

### LAYER 4: FOLLOWS (the value promise and profile-visit-rate)

**4.1 One post converts to a follow only when it implies a repeatable value promise (2025-2026, creator-source).**
Consistent across mirra.my, postoria, Brafton 2025-2026: people follow to keep receiving a specific, repeated PROMISE ("business tips that seem candid," "pastel food photography"). "Even if people enjoy a post, they may not follow unless you give a clear future benefit, such as ongoing education, insights, or resources they won't want to miss." Consequence: a carousel that reads as a one-off does not convert; a carousel that reads as episode 3 of a series the viewer wants more of does. Niche clarity helps both the human ("this account is FOR me") and the algorithm ("the system can understand who the content is for").

**4.2 Profile-visit-rate is a fast-rising 2026 follow/reach signal.**
mirra.my / postoria 2026: "For Feed and carousels, the signal that's risen fastest is profile visit rate. When a single post pushes someone to tap your handle, the model treats that as a strong vote of confidence and shows the next post to a wider seed audience." Two consequences: (1) the carousel should make the viewer want to see MORE from the source (curiosity about the creator, not just the topic), and (2) the profile grid must instantly confirm the promise, "treat your page like a high-converting landing page," or the visit does not convert to a follow. Carter's image-artifact decks (badges, book covers) likely also win here because they make a visually distinctive, recognizable grid that reads as a coherent promise.

**4.3 Expertise-fit is now algorithmically checked (LinkedIn confirmed; IG directional).**
A 2025 finding: LinkedIn now evaluates whether content fits your stated expertise and suppresses off-topic reach. The 2025 Frontiers review and the health-info expertise review (Springer, s13423-026-02943-2) both find source expertise raises persuasiveness AND sharing. For a cyber creator this reinforces staying tightly in-niche: authority-consistent content both converts follows and gets shared more because the sender borrows credible authority.

## SO WHAT FOR US

The mission handed us the paradox baked into Carter's own data: his best decks were SAVE machines (image artifacts, 2,700-6,600 saves) but the 2026 algorithm's KING metric is SENDS. The strategic answer is not to pick one. It is to design decks that are save-worthy AT THE CORE and engineer one deliberate send-trigger moment. Here is how the psychology maps to what we build.

**1. Default the deck to the SAVE archetype: a reference artifact.**
"People do not save entertainment, they save utility." Cheat sheets, tool lists, command references, salary tables, cert roadmaps, before/afters ARE the save-maximizing forms and they are Carter's proven winners. Every carousel should ask: is this a KEEPABLE artifact someone would want findable later? If not, it will not save. The poster-not-newspaper thesis serves this: a clean, single-focal, screenshot-worthy slide reads as a "complete, worth-hoarding reference" and fires the Collector's-Fallacy anxiety-relief save. Busy 6-9-zone newspaper slides read as noise, not an artifact. This is a psychological argument FOR the redesign, not just an aesthetic one.

**2. Name the future moment of need in the save-CTA.**
Self-compassion / future-self research says the save fires harder when the future moment is concrete. Do not write "save this." Write the cyber-specific future scenario: "save this for the night before your Security+ exam," "save this the next time you're staring at a blank terminal," "keep this for your next salary negotiation." Later's research (via multiple 2026 sources) claims explicit save-CTAs on the final slide lift save rate 25-40% (directional, creator-source). We should A/B a named-scenario save-CTA on the final slide of every reference deck.

**3. Engineer ONE send-trigger moment per deck. It is different content from the save core.**
Saves and sends come from different slides (socialmon). To capture the 2026 king metric, add a deliberate send moment:
   - A SELF-PRESENTATION slide the sender forwards to look in-the-know: a genuinely non-obvious tool, an "insider" fact, a myth-bust (Layne-Norton "forward to prove a point" mechanic). Awe or righteous-anger register.
   - A NAMED-RECIPIENT send-ask, never a numeric quota (Meta bans quotas): "send this to the friend who keeps asking how you broke into cyber," "DM this to someone still using the same password everywhere." Give the sender the one-line description of what it is (Currier's friction rule).
   - A relatable/tribal moment: cyber has rich in-group identity (blue vs red team, "it's always DNS," zero-trust). A tribal-signal slide is shareable as identity performance.

**4. Use high-arousal emotional register, kill the low-arousal.**
Awe (a tool that does something jaw-dropping), righteous anger (companies still doing insecure thing X in 2026), and anxiety (the exposure you didn't know you had) are the shareable registers per Berger & Milkman and the 2025 Frontiers review. Neutral, calm, purely-informational framing gets saved but not sent. The cover and the send-slide especially should sit in a high-arousal register.

**5. Every slide is an open loop; the cover opens the biggest gap.**
Loewenstein: attention is a felt deprivation. The cover states one bold thing and withholds resolution (poster, not self-answering newspaper). Interior slides end on partial thoughts the next slide completes. This directly serves the "poster not newspaper" redesign and the swipe-completion the algorithm rewards.

**6. Convert the follow via a legible value promise and a landing-page-grade grid.**
Profile-visit-rate is a rising 2026 signal. The deck should make the viewer want more from CARTER, not just the topic, and the grid must instantly confirm the promise. Carter's distinctive image-artifact visual identity already helps the grid read as a coherent promise; keep that recognizability as we redesign toward posters.

**7. What to measure to know it's working.**
Track saves-per-reach (utility signal, distribution longevity) AND sends-per-reach (the 2026 growth king) as SEPARATE KPIs, not lumped "engagement." Watch the "Shares" line in insights specifically. Watch profile-visit-rate as the follow leading indicator. The hypothesis to validate: poster-style reference decks with one engineered send-moment beat the old busy-newspaper decks on BOTH saves and sends simultaneously.

Bottom line: build a keepable artifact (drives saves, the proven Carter strength and the distribution-longevity signal), inject one high-arousal, named-recipient, identity-signaling send moment (captures the 2026 king metric he is currently leaving on the table), open a real curiosity gap on the cover, and make the profile grid a promise. The redesign toward posters is not a style preference. It is what the save-and-send psychology literally rewards.
