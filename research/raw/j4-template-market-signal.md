- "750+ Canva Instagram Carousel Templates / AI Content Builder" (TheSocialAI) - 15 favorites, "trusted by 2,000+ creators." Background.

Etsy pattern (this is the loud signal):
1. FACELESS is a dominant selling keyword. Buyers explicitly want decks that work without the creator's face, sold as text + stock-photo systems. Repeated across the top listings.
2. Bundles are sold as AESTHETIC lanes, not generic packs: "pink aesthetic," "neutral aesthetic," "scrapbook aesthetic," "purple lavender." Buyers shop by mood/palette.
3. MRR/PLR resale rights (buy once, rebrand, resell) is a huge share of the Etsy carousel economy. Much of this market is people buying templates to RE-SELL, which inflates volume and pushes designs toward safe, generic, on-trend looks.
4. Price band: roughly $19-$25 for the big Canva bundles.
5. Reviews are 5-star and generic ("very quality product that will be usefull for my instagram page"), so the review TEXT carries little design signal; the favorite counts (441, 194) carry the demand signal.

### F. WHAT THE DEMAND-SIGNAL / TREND SOURCES SAY WINS (April-July 2026)

These are the numbers buyers are chasing when they buy these packs. Every stat below was in a fetched page.

- Seamless carousels: ~40% higher completion rate vs standard multi-image posts. Source: slidycreator.com/blog/instagram-carousel-trends (published 2026-04-26).
- Mixed-media (image + video slides): 2.33% engagement vs 1.80% image-only; "highest engagement of any carousel type." Video on slide 1 or 2 = scroll-stopper. Source: creatorflow.so + postnitro.ai, echoed by krumzi.
- Carousels overall: highest engagement rate of any IG format in 2026, ~1.92% vs 0.50% Reels / 0.45% static; ~114% more engagement than single-image. Convert at 12-18% vs bio-link 2-3%. Source: flowshorts.app / marketersclique (via search).
- Krumzi (2026): carousels ~10% avg engagement vs 7% single-image vs 6% Reels; 22% more saves than single photos; DM shares weighted 3-5x higher than likes; saves weighted far above likes.
- One-idea-per-slide is the near-universal rule. "Flashcard, not a blog paragraph." Text under 30 words/slide (some say 15-20). "If a slide looks like a page from a book, the user will bounce." Cover under 8-10 words, one idea not three, make it feel incomplete. Sources: truefuturemedia carousel-strategy-2026; slidycreator; krumzi.
- Named winning visual styles for 2026: bold typography + high contrast on the cover; oversized headline + minimal supporting text (poster, not newspaper); magazine/editorial layouts (serif, high-quality photography, structured grids) for the premium/luxury lane; soft vibrant gradients + glassmorphism + clean sans (Inter, Outfit) for the "high-end app" lane; seamless bleed across slides. Sources: slidycreator, versacreative social-trends-2026, simplified font-trends.

## SO WHAT FOR US

The market VALIDATES Carter's own 90-day data. His image-artifact decks (badge/book-cover imagery, 47-65k views, 2.7-6.6k saves) map exactly to what the paid market sells hardest: one strong visual per slide, poster not newspaper, editorial/magazine framing. His text-only template decks (10-18k views, 100-570 saves) map to the commodity Canva-bundle look that is so oversupplied (814K+ items, MRR/PLR resale flooding) that it is worthless as differentiation. The template market is telling us to run AWAY from the template look.

Concrete implications for what we build in the shotpls pipeline:

1. One focal point per slide is not a style opinion, it is the entire template market's consensus AND the trend data's #1 rule. Our decks should hard-cap one idea per slide, cover under 8-10 words. This is a QUANTIFIED, sourced mandate, not taste.

2. Beat the template look by NOT looking editable. Every paid pack sells "fully customizable, change fonts/colors/photos." That flexibility forces bland, safe, centered layouts. We are a single opinionated brand with a fixed system, so we can do the ONE thing templates structurally can't: extreme, specific, off-grid composition (massive type bleeding off-canvas, one dominant image, brutalist asymmetry). The template market's weakness is our moat.

3. The premium/expensive lane is "magazine/editorial": serif, real photography, structured grid, sophisticated palette, near-zero body copy. The cheap/commodity lane is "text on a soft gradient card." We should live firmly in the editorial-poster lane. That is also where his winning badge/book-cover decks already are.

4. Faceless + image-artifact is the money keyword. Etsy's top favorites (441, 194) and Carter's own top saves both come from decks where a strong ARTIFACT (a stock photo, a badge, a book cover) carries the slide, not the creator's face and not paragraphs. Keep leaning into real artifacts (tool screenshots, cert badges, salary numbers as hero art) as the focal object.

5. Animated / video slides inside the carousel are a real, sold, measured category (CandyMustache AE kit, Motion Array, Envato video-carousel tag) with the best engagement number in the whole sweep (2.33% mixed-media vs 1.80% static). This is the single biggest capability gap for a pure-HTML/shotpls-PNG pipeline. Recommendation: prototype ONE motion slide per deck (an animated cover, or a mid-deck reveal) exported as MP4/WebM and assembled into the IG carousel alongside the PNGs. shotpls gives us PNGs; we would need a small headless-Chrome-to-video step (or an ffmpeg frame-sequence assemble) to ship a video slide. Worth a spike given it is the highest-engagement format in the data.

6. Aesthetic lanes matter to buyers (pink / neutral / scrapbook / editorial). We don't need multiple palettes, but the lesson is that a RECOGNIZABLE, consistent look is what people pay for. Our Synthetic technical-brutalist system should stay ruthlessly consistent slide-to-slide so the feed reads as one brand at a glance, which is exactly the "feed cohesion" pitch every top pack makes.

7. Seamless bleed (elements crossing the slide boundary) is cheap to implement in HTML and worth ~40% completion lift per the data. A rule/line/number/image that visually continues from slide N to N+1 is a low-effort, high-signal upgrade to our decks.

8. Price/positioning aside note (not our product, but useful): the entire paid carousel-template market tops out around $29 for a premium single-designer pack and $19-$25 for big Canva bundles. That is the ceiling on what a "template" is worth. Our advantage is that we are not selling templates, we are building a brand, so we can invest FAR more design effort per slide than any $29 pack can amortize. Out-design the market by out-caring per slide.

Bottom line: the template market's best sellers converge on exactly the poster-not-newspaper, one-focal-point, editorial, artifact-led direction Wave 2 is pushing us toward, and Carter's own top-performing decks already sit there. The differentiator vs the template flood is opinionated, non-editable, extreme composition plus a motion-slide spike.
