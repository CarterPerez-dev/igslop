## FINDINGS

### 1. The algorithm now ranks carousels on SWIPE-THROUGH, DWELL, and SENDS, not likes

The single most repeated 2026 claim across sources: Instagram's three top signals are watch
time, sends per reach (DM shares), and likes per reach. This is attributed to Adam Mosseri and
quoted near-identically on truefuturemedia (2026-01-22): "watch time, likes per reach, and sends
per reach (how often content gets shared via DM)."

Carousel-specific mechanics that follow from that:
- DM shares (sends) are described as weighted "3-5x higher than likes" in the 2026 algorithm
  (repeated on truefuturemedia, thesecondbrain, creatorflow, marketingagent). thesecondbrain
  (2026-04-18) hedges it as "roughly three to five times heavier," saves at "~2x the weight of
  likes," comments at "~2x."
- Carousels with high swipe-through (70%+ of slides viewed) are said to be distributed to "3-5x
  more non-followers" than low-swipe carousels (truefuturemedia carousel-strategy, 2026-01-29).
- "Total Dwell Time" and "Completion Rate" are called the new primary ranking signals for
  carousels; 7-10 slide posts generate "3.5x more dwell time" than single images (marketingagent,
  cross-cited). This is a vendor stat, not a Meta stat.
- Re-serve / second-chance mechanic: if a user does not finish a carousel, Instagram reshows it
  later starting on unseen slides, so one carousel earns engagement over 24-48 hours, not one hour
  (carouselli 2026-04-30, multiple others). Re-serve window quoted as 24-48h.

Carousel-vs-format baselines seen (all vendor-sourced, ranges disagree, cite as directional):
- Carousels ~1.4x reach vs static, attributed to Mosseri "as of February 2026" (multiple blogs).
- truefuturemedia reach page: carousel avg engagement 10.15%, single images ~7%, Reels ~6%.
  Slide-1 exit rate 23.8%; slide 4+ exit rate ~15%.
- thesecondbrain: carousel engagement ~9-10% vs Reels ~6-7%; carousel save rate ~2x Reels;
  carousel DM-share rate ~2-3x Reels; time-per-post carousels 15-45s vs Reels 8-15s. BUT Reels
  reach 3-5x more accounts and ~10x more views. Net: Reels for discovery, carousels for
  engagement/saves/depth.
- Format split by follower count (thesecondbrain): <5K lean 70/30 Reels; 5-10K 50/50; >10K lean
  60/40 carousels.

TAKEAWAY FRAME: the deck's job is to be SWIPED ALL THE WAY THROUGH and SENT to one person. Every
copy decision serves swipe-completion and shareability first, likes never.

### 2. HOW MUCH TEXT PER SLIDE: top performers use dramatically LESS than we do

This is the clearest and most consistent finding, and it directly indicts the "6-9 reading zones
per slide" problem.

Numbers, by source:
- "The new standard is micro-learning: 15-20 words maximum per slide, paired with a strong visual
  anchor" (truefuturemedia carousel-strategy). Same source: two years ago creators "packed as
  much value as possible into every slide," now "mobile users have even less patience."
- Cover text limit: 8-10 words max, quoted independently by truefuturemedia AND the influencer
  roundup ("keep it under 8-10 words"). One headline + one subhead maximum per slide.
- carouselli (2026-04-30): "one idea per slide," maximum of TWO short sentences per slide.
- Alternate ceiling: 30-50 words max per slide (looser sources). Body font floor 24pt because
  80%+ browse on phones; paragraphs kept to 1-2 sentences.
- Scrolling speed cited as justification: users average "3-4 posts per second," first slide must
  win in "the first half-second" / "1-2 seconds" (truefuturemedia).

Interpretation for the newspaper problem: the meta says ONE focal point per slide. A slide with
hud row + kicker + multi-line headline + rule + paragraph + tag row + stat block + pager + button
is 6-9 zones where the meta calls for 1 headline + at most 1 subhead. Our decks are 3-9x over the
current text budget per slide.

### 3. HOOK PATTERNS getting reach: the six-category model + 30 real examples

Every serious source converges on a 6-bucket psychological model. Two near-identical taxonomies:
- truefuturemedia: curiosity, FOMO, greed, defensiveness, relief, disbelief.
- resont/contentdrips: curiosity/information-gap, loss aversion, credibility, narrative
  compulsion (story/vulnerability), contrarian reflex, value exchange (promise/how-to).

Underlying formula (contentdrips 2026-06-10): "[Who this is for] + [The gap or tension] + [The
payoff]." Hooks should be ONE to TWO sentences, as short as possible while still creating tension.
"You have 2-3 seconds on the first slide."

Cover-slide construction (multiple sources): BOLD HOOK HEADLINE (5-8 words, largest text on
slide) + VISUAL PATTERN INTERRUPT (high-contrast color, expressive face, or dramatic graphic) +
CURIOSITY TRIGGER (partial reveal / surprising stat / open question unresolvable without swiping).

Curiosity theory named explicitly: Loewenstein's information-gap theory. The named-destination
hook ("Wait until slide 7") is called out as directly rewarded because it plants a reward at a
specific reachable slide, mechanically lifting swipe-through.

### 4. SAVE / SHARE / DM CTA conventions (exact phrasings)

Save CTAs (verbatim):
- "Save this for the next time you ___." (truefuturemedia, fill-in-the-blank pattern)
- "Save this for later" (carouselli, called generic/baseline)
- "Save this post: 30 days of..." (contentdrips, save-instruction baked INTO the hook)

Share/send CTAs (verbatim) - these are the reach lever given sends are top-weighted:
- "Send this to the person who handles ___." (truefuturemedia)
- "tag someone who needs to hear this" (truefuturemedia reach)

DM-trigger CTA (verbatim):
- "DM 'CHECKLIST' and I'll send the template." (truefuturemedia carousel-strategy)

Comment CTA that beats generic (truefuturemedia reach):
- Bad: "let me know in comments." Better: "comment with the number of the tip you will try
  first" or "comment [KEYWORD]." Specific CTAs said to outperform generic by ~3x.

Other baseline CTAs: "Follow for more on X," "Link in bio to try this free" (carouselli).

CONVENTION SUMMARY: best decks pair TWO asks: a save ("save this checklist") plus a send ("send
this to the person who handles it"). Sources explicitly recommend the double CTA because save
signals value-to-self and send is the 3-5x reach multiplier. Put the CTA on the LAST slide AND
repeat the send-ask in the caption on its own isolated line.

### 5. CAPTION STRUCTURE + KEYWORD SEO (this is now a real ranking surface)

Caption is no longer decoration. Google and Bing now index public professional-account Instagram
captions, and captions drive in-app search. Named tactics:

Structure (multiple sources, consistent):
- First line = standalone hook = sharpest insight or provocative claim. Only line 1 (roughly
  first 125 characters) shows before "more." Put primary keyword in first 1-2 sentences.
- Lines 2-5 = 3-4 sentences of value expansion that does NOT duplicate the on-slide text.
  Caption should EXTEND the carousel, never repeat it (carouselli).
- CTA on its OWN isolated line with whitespace above it. "Single-line CTAs convert far better
  than CTAs glued to the end of a paragraph."
- Educational-carousel caption sweet spot: 150-300 words (some sources say "under 30 words" for
  the visible portion; reconcile as: hook line short, total caption 150-300w).
- Short paragraphs, 2-3 lines each, line breaks for scannability.

Keyword / hashtag SEO (2026 state):
- Hashtags "dramatically deprioritized," now a categorization tool not a discovery driver.
  Recommended 3-5 highly relevant hashtags (some tests cap at 3). The 30-hashtag era is dead.
- Keyword-rich captions reportedly generate "~30% more reach and twice as many likes" vs
  hashtag-heavy posts (lamplightcreatives / socialrealtr, snippet-sourced).
- Keywords belong in captions, bio, alt text, AND on-screen slide text. Alt text up to 100 chars,
  keyword the "who/what/where." On-slide text is treated as searchable phrases (truefuturemedia).
- Semantic mapping matters: use varied related terms across slides (cover=topic, slide2=subtopic,
  etc.) to signal niche authority, not just keyword repetition.
- Meta guidance quoted: put keywords/hashtags in CAPTIONS not comments if you want search reach.

NEW FEATURE TO WATCH: Instagram is testing PER-SLIDE captions (almcorp 2026-05-12) letting each
carousel slide carry its own caption/keywords. Still limited test, no numbers, but if it ships it
turns each slide into an independent SEO/semantic surface. Design implication: our per-slide HTML
files should carry a machine-readable topic line we can lift straight into a per-slide caption.

### 6. COMMENT-KEYWORD DM FUNNEL HEALTH in 2026: alive but rate-clamped and report-sensitive

The funnel still works and is still standard, but 2026 tightened the rules hard. Facts, mostly
from creatorflow DM-compliance (2026-07-04) and the DM-backlash search:

Still allowed / still works:
- Comment-to-DM via trigger keyword works on feed posts AND Reels; target one post, all, or the
  next one. ManyChat is a Meta-approved Tech Provider using the official Graph API (not scraping).
- DM funnels claim 80%+ open rates because the message lands in the primary inbox.

2026 hard limits (NEW, load-bearing):
- 200 automated DMs per hour per IG account (rolling 60-min window, all tools combined).
- 1 automated DM per user per 24 hours from comment or Story triggers (NEW in 2026). Kills the
  "same user gets DMed from 3 different posts" pattern.
- Instagram cut Graph API limits from ~5,000 calls/hour to ~200/hour (quoted as a ~96% reduction)
  in one snippet; treat exact figure as directional, direction is "much stricter."
- First DM after a comment trigger: only ONE content block allowed (single text OR image with
  buttons). The opening DM MUST be short. No multi-part welcome.
- Report-rate threshold: reports above 0.6% of recipients trigger automated review.
- Fingerprint/spam detector: sending the exact same message text to >25 recipients within an hour
  is a top violation; the detector is sensitive to character-level repetition INCLUDING emoji and
  link slugs (across ~380 tracked coach accounts).
- Message tags CONFIRMED_EVENT_UPDATE, ACCOUNT_UPDATE, POST_PURCHASE_UPDATE deprecated
  2026-04-27 (return error 100); must migrate to Utility Templates / Marketing Messages API.
- Personal accounts have NO API access; must be Creator/Business connected to a FB Page.
- Must include opt-out ("Reply STOP") and stay in the 24-hour window for promo.

Health verdict: the comment-keyword DM funnel is NOT dying, but it is now a compliance surface.
The design consequence: vary the opening-DM text (do not ship identical strings at scale), keep
first DM to one block, and do not over-DM the same follower across a posting week. The "comment
KEYWORD" CTA on the last slide is still legitimate and still the primary lead-capture move.

### 7. TEXT-HEAVY vs VISUAL-FIRST: visual-first is clearly rising, text-heavy is falling

Direction is unambiguous across sources, though "text-heavy" survives in one specific mode.

Evidence text-heavy educational density is FALLING:
- "Two years ago, creators packed as much value as possible into every slide. Today, mobile users
  have even less patience. The new standard is micro-learning: 15-20 words max per slide, paired
  with a strong visual anchor." (truefuturemedia)
- "Instead of writing a paragraph about a concept, successful creators use diagrams, charts, or
  simple before-and-after visuals." Slides overloaded with text "reduce attention and
  comprehension."
- "Instagram in 2026 rewards visual excellence and rapid value delivery... minimal text, seamless
  designs, premium aesthetics."
- Design-trend sources (kittl, adobe express, made-by-dave) all frame 2026 as visual-first,
  premium, minimal.

Where text-heavy STILL WINS (the nuance): a minimalist, one-line-per-slide, high-contrast style
that is text-DRIVEN but not text-DENSE. The viral-carousel roundup (amraandelma, dated 2025-08-10,
background) names the exact operators doing this: Jay Shetty (@jayshetty, minimalist quote slides),
Steven Bartlett (@steven, "minimalist, text-heavy" podcast-quote slides), Justin Welsh
(@thejustinwelsh, "bold, minimal designs"), Alex Hormozi (@hormozi, "sharp, high-contrast slides,
bold claims then step-by-step"), Ali Abdaal (@aliabdaal, "simple, legible" frameworks), Sahil
Bloom (@sahilbloom, "clean, consistent"), Chris Do (@thechrisdo, "clear progression, consistent
layout"). Note these are follower-count-sorted background, not fresh performance data, but the
STYLE consensus (big type, one idea, consistent template, near-zero body copy) matches every 2026
copy source above.

So the correct read is NOT "text is dead." It is: text is the design, but it is ONE line, huge,
per slide. Text-heavy-in-density (paragraphs, multi-zone) is falling; text-as-poster (one giant
line) is exactly what the named winners ship.

### 8. ANIMATED / MIXED-MEDIA SLIDES are a live edge (copy implication: even less text)

Relevant to the "animated slides inside carousels" goal:
- Mixed-media carousels (static images + short video clips) reportedly hit the HIGHEST engagement
  rate at ~2.33% vs ~1.92% carousel average, and generate "114% more total engagement" (creatorflow
  carousel-guide, snippet). Adding even one or two 5-second clips to an otherwise static carousel
  is said to boost performance because video lifts dwell time, a top signal.
- Adding MUSIC to a carousel can push it into the Reels feed for wider reach (multiple sources).

Copy implication: an animated slide carries even LESS static text than a static slide because
motion is the focal point. If we add video slides, treat their text as a single kinetic line, and
lean on the still slides to carry the (already minimal) reading.

## SO WHAT FOR US (how this changes what we build)

1. KILL the newspaper. The meta budget is 1 headline + at most 1 subhead per slide (8-10 words
   cover, 15-20 words max interior, 2 short sentences absolute ceiling). Our 6-9 reading-zone
   slides are 3-9x over budget. The redesign is not "trim," it is "one focal point per slide."
   Collapse hud row / kicker / tag row / stat block / pager / button into at most ONE supporting
   element. This is the top structural change.

2. Build a HOOK-COVER generator, not a title slide. Cover = 5-8 word hook (largest type on the
   deck) + one pattern-interrupt visual + a curiosity trigger. Bake in the 6-category model and
   seed it with the 30 verbatim examples above, remapped to our lanes (job market, salary,
   certifications, tools, mistakes). Strong candidates to adapt: "Your resume doesn't get you
   hired. This does." / "I got rejected by 47 companies before landing my dream job." / "Stop
   saying these 5 things in salary negotiations." / "The [security] advice everyone gives is
   actively hurting your career." Always full word "certification," never "cert" (house rule).

3. Add a NAMED-DESTINATION mechanic to multi-slide decks. "Wait until slide 7" style planting a
   reward at a specific reachable slide mechanically lifts swipe-through, which is now the ranking
   input. Bake a "the [payoff] is on slide N" pointer into the cover or slide 2 when the deck has
   a genuine payoff slide.

4. Make the LAST slide a DOUBLE CTA: save + send, on separate lines. Save-ask ("save this
   [cheat-sheet]") plus send-ask ("send this to the [person] still doing X"). Send is the 3-5x
   reach lever. Keep the "comment KEYWORD" DM-trigger CTA as the lead-capture variant, but rotate
   the wording (compliance, see #6).

5. Treat the CAPTION as a first-class deliverable of the carousel pipeline, not an afterthought.
   Auto-generate: line-1 hook with the primary keyword in the first ~125 chars, 150-300 word value
   body that EXTENDS (never repeats) the slides, isolated single-line CTA, 3-5 relevant hashtags,
   plus keyword-rich alt text (<=100 chars). Our HTML-per-slide files should each emit a
   machine-readable topic line so we can (a) fill alt text and (b) be ready for per-slide captions
   if that test ships.

6. Tighten the DM funnel for 2026 compliance IN THE TOOLING: never ship identical opening-DM
   strings at scale (>25 identical/hour flags), keep first DM to one content block, one DM per
   user per 24h, include "Reply STOP." If we template DM copy, template it with rotation slots.

7. Prototype ANIMATED/MIXED slides. Since our render path is standalone HTML to shotpls PNG, add
   a variant that renders a short video slide (or an APNG/MP4 export) for 1-2 slides per deck.
   Mixed-media reportedly tops engagement (~2.33% vs 1.92%) and lifts dwell. Music pushes the deck
   toward Reels distribution. Even one 5s motion slide is the cited threshold. Animated slides
   carry even less text: one kinetic line.

8. Optimize for SWIPE-COMPLETION and SENDS as the north-star metrics, not likes. Every copy and
   layout call should answer "does this get finished and forwarded to one specific person." That
   reframes the whole deck: cover earns the swipe, each interior slide earns the NEXT swipe (open
   a loop, close it one slide later), last slide earns the send.

BOTTOM LINE: the current meta and our stated goal ("posters not newspapers") are the same target.
The winners ship ONE giant line per slide, a hook cover built on a named psychological trigger, a
save+send double CTA, and a keyword-loaded caption that extends the deck. Our advantage is the
HTML-to-PNG pipeline: we can programmatically enforce the word budget, generate the caption+alt
text as part of the build, seed hooks from the 6-category bank, and add motion slides that most
template-tool competitors cannot.
