## FINDINGS

### 1. Best posting times for education (two independent datasets agree)

VERIFIED, cross-corroborated by two of the largest public datasets in the space.

**Buffer (updated May 14, 2026):** 9.6 million Instagram posts, 200,000+ accounts, Jan 2024 to Dec 2025. Methodology used MEDIAN engagement rate = (likes + comments + shares + saves / followers) x 100 so big accounts do not skew it. Findings:
- Top three overall slots: Thursday 9am, Wednesday 12pm, Wednesday 6pm.
- Best day: Wednesday, then Thursday, then Tuesday. Worst days: Friday and Saturday.
- Evening 6pm to 11pm is strongest on every weekday EXCEPT Thursday.
- Carousel-specific note (Buffer's own framing, not Meta): Wednesday 12pm, Wednesday 6pm, Thursday 9am work for carousels because "readers have the time to swipe through."
- Buffer gives NO education/business niche breakdown. (Gap.)
- Source: https://buffer.com/resources/when-is-the-best-time-to-post-on-instagram/

**Sprout Social (2026):** ~2 billion engagements across ~307,000 global profiles, window Nov 27, 2025 to Feb 27, 2026, times in local time. Findings:
- Best overall windows: Mon 2 to 4pm, Tue 1 to 7pm, Wed 12 to 9pm (plus an 11pm spike), Thu 12 to 2pm.
- EDUCATION INDUSTRY SPECIFIC: "Tuesdays and Wednesdays at 11 a.m. perform best for schools and educational institutions." This is the single most on-target datapoint we found for our niche.
- Worst days: Saturday and Sunday, lowest across nearly all industries.
- Source: https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-instagram/

Convergence: both datasets independently land on Tuesday/Wednesday as the strongest days for professional/education content, and both bury the weekend. Sprout's education-specific 11am Tue/Wed and Buffer's Wed 12pm are effectively the same midday-weekday pocket. High confidence in "Tue or Wed late morning to midday" as the education slot.

Caveat every source repeats: your own Insights beat any benchmark. Cybersecurity-cert audience skews working professionals + students, which fits the education/professional midday pattern more than the entertainment evening pattern.

### 2. First-hour / early-engagement mechanics (velocity is real, "first hour" as a hard rule is folklore)

The nuance matters. What is officially supported vs what is repeated blog lore:

OFFICIALLY SUPPORTED (Instagram/Mosseri via Buffer's summary of Meta statements):
- Instagram's feed ranking uses engagement history, post performance (likes, comments, shares, saves AND "speed of engagement"), creator credibility (how many people interacted with the creator in the past few weeks), and relationship history. "Speed of engagement" is the closest official acknowledgment that velocity matters. Source: https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-algorithms/
- Mosseri's five monitored actions: how likely you are to spend a few seconds on a post, comment, like, share, and tap the profile photo.
- Buffer's read of the official position: Instagram does NOT describe an explicit "audition" phase or a hard first-hour window. That framing is creator-blog synthesis, not a Meta statement.

FOLKLORE (widely repeated, NOT traceable to a Meta statement, treat as directional only):
- "Posts that get likes/comments/shares in the first 30 to 60 minutes get pushed to a wider audience; the same engagement spread over days does not." Repeated by creatorflow, marketingagent.blog, truefuturemedia. No primary source.
- "If followers engage heavily within the first hour, Instagram tests it with a small Explore audience." Same status.
- The "audition system": post gets shown to a small non-follower group, if it performs it expands, repeated in waves. This is a decent mental model and matches how Trial Reels demonstrably work, but Meta has not published it as the general-feed mechanism.

Sprout's own framing (their advice, not a Meta quote) is the cleanest statement of the real principle: "engagement velocity, or how quickly users interact with your content right after it goes live, dictates algorithmic success," and "recency dictates your initial reach" while relevance dictates sustained reach.

Bottom line: velocity of early engagement is real and officially acknowledged as "speed of engagement." The specific "first 60 minutes or it dies" numbers are unverified folklore. Posting into an active audience window matters because it front-loads velocity, not because a literal 60-minute timer exists.

### 3. Does Instagram trial carousels on non-followers like reels? NO, not as of July 2026

VERIFIED with a clear answer.
- Trial Reels (official Instagram feature): show a reel to NON-FOLLOWERS FIRST, not to your followers or your grid. Announced Dec 10, 2024. Requires a public account with 1,000+ followers. After ~24 hours you get views/likes/comments/shares. Auto-share option: Instagram will push a strong trial to your followers automatically based on views in the first 72 hours. In FEBRUARY 2026 Instagram added scheduling of Trial Reels so you can batch a week of tests. Sources: https://creators.instagram.com/blog/instagram-trial-reels and https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-allows-creators-to-schedule-trial-reels/816549/
- The official Instagram Trial Reels page makes ZERO mention of carousels. Confirmed by direct fetch.
- Multiple 2026 sources searched specifically for carousel trials: none document Instagram testing carousels with non-followers before showing followers. The feature is reels-only right now.

So there is NO native "test this carousel on non-followers first" button. That capability does not exist for carousels in mid-2026. If we want a pre-test signal for a carousel concept, the only native proxy is to run the same HOOK/cover as a Trial Reel first, read the 72-hour non-follower signal, then build the winning concept as a carousel.

What DOES apply to carousels natively: Instagram RE-SERVES carousels to people who scrolled past, starting them on a slide they did not see. Repeated across truefuturemedia, marketingagent.blog, contentdrips. This is not a trial, it is passive resurfacing, and it is why non-first slides still need to earn a stop. This gives carousels a longer-than-reels engagement tail (days, not the reels-style front-loaded burst).

### 4. Boosting / paid amplification of organic winners

VERIFIED mechanics, secondary-source cost data.
- The correct play everyone agrees on: do NOT boost cold. Boost content that ALREADY won organically. Metricool: "Boosting low-engagement posts is like yelling into the void. Start with your best... this feature generally works best on content that is already performing well." Source: https://metricool.com/boost-instagram-posts/
- Which signals mark a boostable winner: high saves and high DM shares specifically (not likes). Saves = the algorithm reads it as revisit-worthy; sends = it travels to non-followers. Carousels are the highest-save format, which makes our winning image-artifact decks (2,700 to 6,600 saves) the exact profile you would boost.
- Boost button vs Ads Manager: Boost = quick, post-level, basic analytics, approval usually under 24 hours. Ads Manager = full targeting, placements, formats, deep analytics. For amplifying one organic winner, Boost is the right tool; for a real campaign, Ads Manager.
- Cost figures (Metricool, directional, global averages): CPM $8 to $14; cost-per-link-click $0.70 to $1.20; minimum daily budget technically $1 to $5 but "meaningful data" needs $1,000 to $2,000/month for testing. These are agency-scale numbers, higher than a solo creator needs to validate a single post.
- Case-study numbers cited by dlvrit: a tourism brand hit +189% reach combining organic with minimal boosting; a retail brand hit +40% impressions using carousels plus Stories promotion. Secondary, uncontrolled, treat as illustrative not benchmark.
- Strategic split repeated across sources: 60 to 70% of budget on organic content creation, 30 to 40% on ads amplifying the proven winners.

### 5. Cadence: daily vs 3x weekly (2026 data says 3 to 5x weekly beats daily for growth)

VERIFIED, backed by named datasets. This contradicts the old "post every day" gospel.
- Buffer/Iconosquare 2026 (100,000+ accounts): 3 to 5 feed posts per week is optimal for most accounts. 52% of top-performing accounts post 4x/week. Biggest reach-per-post jump comes moving from 1 to 2 posts up to 3 to 5 (~12% lift). Source cluster: buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-instagram/ and buffer.com/resources/social-media-frequency-guide/
- Overposting penalty: accounts posting MORE than 7x/week see ~25% higher unfollow rates unless content is highly segmented. Daily-plus is a net negative for most.
- Underposting penalty: accounts posting fewer than 3x/week see ~40% lower reach from infrequent algorithm signals. Zero-post weeks correlate with follower stagnation or loss.
- CONSISTENCY beats raw frequency: Buffer's State of Social study (52 million posts, 220,000 accounts, 26 weeks) found accounts that posted in 20+ of 26 weeks saw a 450% engagement lift vs accounts posting in 4 weeks or fewer. Showing up every week for months matters more than hitting a high daily number.

Note: several of the sharper cadence numbers (25% unfollow, 40% reach drop, 12% lift) come from aggregator blogs summarizing Buffer/Iconosquare rather than the primary studies. The DIRECTION (3 to 5x/week sweet spot, daily-plus counterproductive, consistency over 20+ weeks is the real driver) is well-supported. The exact percentages are softer.

### 6. Mosseri ranking-signal hierarchy (for what to design the deck to earn)

Mixed verification. What is solidly attributable vs blog-amplified:
- SOLID: Mosseri has repeatedly stated shares/sends are among the most important distribution signals, and specifically that DM shares matter MORE for reaching NON-FOLLOWERS while likes matter more for existing followers. Social Media Today covered Instagram's own 2025 algorithm-insights release. Sources: https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/ and later.com/blog/how-instagram-algorithm-works/
- SOLID-ish: the three-signal shorthand "watch time, sends per reach, likes per reach" appears across Buffer, Later, dataslayer, creatorflow as Mosseri-confirmed. For carousels "watch time" reads as time-on-post / swipe-through dwell.
- FOLKLORE / UNVERIFIED NUMBER: "sends per reach is weighted 3 to 5x higher than likes." This exact multiplier is cited everywhere (influencermarketinghub, truefuturemedia, contentdrips) attributed to a Mosseri Q&A, but I could NOT find the primary Mosseri source stating "3 to 5x." Treat the DIRECTION (sends >> likes for non-follower reach) as verified and the specific 3-5x number as unconfirmed folklore.

### 7. Originality policy now covers carousels (April 30, 2026) — direct relevance to our repost cadence

VERIFIED by multiple tier-1 outlets on the same date.
- On April 30, 2026 Instagram expanded its originality/anti-aggregator enforcement from Reels to ALSO cover PHOTOS and CAROUSELS. Confirmed by TechCrunch, PetaPixel, Tubefilter, eMarketer, Dataconomy. Sources: https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/30/instagram-restricts-reach-of-content-aggregators-in-new-crackdown/ and https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/
- Penalty: accounts that regularly repost content they did not create, or did not meaningfully transform, LOSE eligibility for recommendations to non-followers (feed recommendations and the Discover/Explore surfaces). Followers can still see the content directly.
- What counts as original per Instagram: wholly created content, or material edits that add humor, commentary, unique text, creative edits, voiceover. Watermarks, credits, speed changes, and screenshots-with-visible-credit do NOT count as transformation.
- Recovery: TechCrunch's writeup did not state a 30-day rule, but several 2026 follow-ups (Tubefilter, gotmenow) describe a rolling 30-day window: if most of your recent posts are original over that period, the account regains recommendation eligibility. Treat "rolling ~30-day recovery" as widely reported but not in the TechCrunch primary.

This is not a reposting risk for us (our decks are original), but it is a tailwind: aggregators reposting screenshots of cheat-sheets now get throttled off Explore, which raises the ceiling for genuinely original educational carousels like ours.

## SO WHAT FOR US

Concrete changes to the build + posting workflow, tied to the findings above.

1. LOCK the posting window to the education pocket. Default to Tuesday or Wednesday, late morning to midday, in the audience's dominant US time zone (Sprout: Tue/Wed 11am for education; Buffer: Wed 12pm for carousels). Secondary slot Thursday 9am to noon. Never default to weekends for a value/education deck. Confirm against our own Insights after 4 to 6 posts. This is the single highest-confidence, zero-effort lever in this division.

2. Stop optimizing for a literal "first hour." Optimize for engagement VELOCITY, which just means posting into an already-active window (see #1) and seeding the swipe. The "post at exactly X or it dies in 60 min" advice is unverified folklore. What is real: recency drives initial reach, then relevance and sends/saves drive the tail. Do not build a ritual around a 60-minute timer.

3. There is NO native carousel trial. We cannot A/B a carousel on non-followers before followers see it. Two implications: (a) the cover slide has to be right on the first shot because there is no low-risk pre-test, and (b) if we want a pre-signal, run the deck's HOOK LINE as a Trial Reel first (non-followers, 24 to 72h read), and only invest in the full deck for hooks that clear the trial. This makes the reel/carousel pipeline complementary: reel as the cheap non-follower probe, carousel as the save engine once the hook is proven.

4. Design every slide to be a valid re-entry point. Instagram re-serves carousels starting on slides users have not seen. So no slide can be filler that only makes sense after slide 1. Each slide should read as a standalone poster (which is exactly the poster-not-newspaper direction of this whole operation). This is an algorithmic argument for the redesign, not just an aesthetic one.

5. Design for SENDS and SAVES, in that order, because those are the two signals that (a) travel to non-followers and (b) mark a post as boostable. Sends >> likes for reaching new people (verified direction, unverified 3-5x number). A poster-style slide with one quotable, screenshot-and-send-worthy line (a salary number, a brutal one-liner, a single tool name) is engineered for the send. Our image-artifact winners already prove this: 2,700 to 6,600 saves on the badge/book decks vs 100 to 570 on text-template decks.

6. Boosting policy: never boost cold, only amplify proven organic winners, and pick winners by SAVES and SENDS, not likes. Our image-artifact decks (47k to 65k views, thousands of saves) are the exact boostable profile. Start small ($5 to $20 as a solo creator, ignore the agency $1,000+ figures) on a deck that already over-indexed on saves in its first 48 hours. Use the Boost button, not Ads Manager, for single-post amplification.

7. Cadence: target 3 to 5 posts per week, NOT daily. Daily-plus (7x+/week) correlates with higher unfollows for un-segmented accounts. The real growth driver is consistency across 20+ weeks (Buffer: 450% engagement lift), not cramming a high daily number. For our mix, that means a sustainable weekly slate (the existing 7-reel + 7-carousel weekly system may be at the edge of the overposting zone if it all hits one account: consider whether carousels + reels combined exceed 7/week on the main handle, and if so lean the carousel side toward the 3 to 5 highest-effort poster decks rather than volume).

8. The April 30, 2026 originality crackdown is a tailwind, not a risk, for us. Cheat-sheet screenshot aggregators now lose Explore reach. Our original, designed decks are exactly what the policy is trying to elevate. No action needed except: keep everything original (we do) and know the competitive floor for lazy reposters just dropped.

## GAPS / DEAD ENDS

- No dataset gives a saves-per-slide or sends-per-slide benchmark specifically for the cybersecurity or IT-cert sub-niche. Everything is "education/schools" broad. Our own Insights are the only source of truth at niche resolution.
- Could not locate the PRIMARY Mosseri source for the "sends weighted 3 to 5x likes" multiplier. Direction verified; exact number is blog folklore.
- No evidence Instagram is building carousel trials. Absence of evidence, not proof it is not in testing. Worth re-checking each quarter.
- Buffer's carousel-specific timing is Buffer's interpretation, not a Meta statement. Meta does not publish format-specific best times.
- Boosting cost/ROI figures are agency-scale secondary data (Metricool, dlvrit case studies), not creator-scale controlled experiments. No clean "boost a $20 carousel, get X saves" number exists in public sources.
- The sharper cadence percentages (25% unfollow above 7x, 40% reach drop below 3x, 12% lift) are aggregator restatements of Buffer/Iconosquare, not quotes I could pin to the primary studies. Trust the direction, discount the decimals.
