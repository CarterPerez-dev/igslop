## FINDINGS

### 1. The Vignelli Canon (primary source, fetched and read in full) [BACKGROUND, author died 2014, text is the closest thing to a rulebook for exactly our problem]

Source: Massimo Vignelli, "The Vignelli Canon," free PDF hosted by the RIT Vignelli Center, https://www.rit.edu/vignellicenter/sites/rit.edu.vignellicenter/files/documents/The%20Vignelli%20Canon.pdf . I downloaded the 3.9MB PDF, ran pdftotext, and read the Tangibles section directly. These are exact quotes, not summaries.

THE TWO-SIZE RULE (this is the single most useful rule for us). From the "Contrasting Type Sizes" section: "Another element is the relationship among type sizes in the same printed page. Our first rule is to stick to one or two type sizes at the most. If necessary, there are other devices such as bold, light, roman and italic to differentiate different parts of a text, but even there, stick to the minimum." He restates it in "Type Size Relationship": "Basically we stick to no more then two type sizes on a printed page, but there are exceptions."

HIERARCHY THROUGH SCALE CONTRAST, NOT SCREAMING. From "Contrasting Type Sizes": "One of the most exciting elements of typography for me is the contrast of scale on a printed page. I love the play between a very large type size for headlines versus a much smaller type size for the body text, with proper white space in between." And the money quote against our current "busy" decks: "Some people who talk loud and tend to scream trying to persuade you, love to increase the size and weight of type to make the message louder. That is exactly what I consider intellectual vulgarity. In a world where everybody screams, silence is noticeable. White space provides the silence."

WHITESPACE IS THE PROTAGONIST. From the "White Space" section: "In typography the white space is more important than the black of the type." And from "Layouts": "Ultimately the most important device is the management of the white space in the layouts. It is the white space that makes the layout sing. Bad layouts have no space left for breathing, every little space covered by a cacophony of type-sizes, images, and screaming titles." That last sentence is a near-verbatim description of our current carousels.

THE GRID IS INVISIBLE INFRASTRUCTURE. From "Grids, Margins, Columns and Modules": the grid exists "to help to prevent arbitrary, meaningless placements of the information on the printed page." His analogy elsewhere: the grid is like underwear, you wear it but do not expose it. And on visibility: "We like the layouts that almost disappear to the eyes of the reader. We would say that if you see the layout, it is probably a bad layout."

FEW TYPEFACES. He ran an entire exhibition proving a huge range of printed matter using only four typefaces: Garamond, Bodoni, Century Expanded, Helvetica. Quote: "It is not the type but what you do with it that counts. The accent was on structure rather than type." He tolerates about a dozen faces total across a career.

TIGHT VS WIDE MARGINS AS A TENSION DIAL. From "White Space": "Tight margins establish a tension between text, images and the edges of the page. Wider margins deflate the tension and bring about a certain level of serenity to the page." So margin width is a deliberate emotional control, not a default.

KERNING AS EXPRESSION. Same section: "Tight type setting transforms words into lines just as loose type settings transform words into dots. Decreasing or increasing the letter spacing confers very distinctive character and expression to the words."

### 2. Swiss / International Typographic Style core rules [BACKGROUND]

Sources: PRINT Magazine "Swiss Style: The Principles, the Typefaces & the Designers" (https://www.printmag.com/featured/swiss-style-principles-typefaces-designers/); MasterClass "Swiss Design: 5 Elements" (https://www.masterclass.com/articles/swiss-design); Poster House "The Swiss Grid" (https://swissgrid.posterhouse.org/).

- Everything starts from a mathematically calculated grid. The grid is "the most legible and harmonious means for structuring information." It is an invisible framework enforcing consistency and hierarchy.
- Hierarchy is built from four levers only: SIZE, WEIGHT, COLOR, SPACING. Used to create "unambiguous hierarchy."
- Layout is ASYMMETRIC. Text is flush-left / ragged-right, never centered (centering is reserved for rhetorical or lapidary text per Vignelli).
- Whitespace is a deliberate choice to eliminate visual noise and pull the eye to the single most important element. Objectivity and clarity over decoration.

### 3. Josef Muller-Brockmann grid systems [BACKGROUND]

Source: "Grid Systems in Graphic Design" (1961/1968), summaries at designyourway.net and the archived full PDF (archive.org GridSystemsInGraphicDesignJosefMullerBrockmann). Key principles:
- "The grid system is an aid, not a guarantee." It is a structural, systematic basis, not a formula that makes design good on its own.
- White space is a FUNCTIONAL element, not emptiness to fill. "Space created rhythm and emphasized content hierarchy." Every element must have a purpose.
- Grid gives proportional relationships between elements and guides attention to what matters.

### 4. Type-scale ratios and the maximum number of hierarchy levels [BACKGROUND theory, sourced from current articles]

Sources: Toptal "How to Structure an Effective Typographic Hierarchy" (https://www.toptal.com/designers/typography/typographic-hierarchy); Cieden type-scale book (https://cieden.com/book/sub-atomic/typography/different-type-scale-types); Digital Polo "Proportion in Design (2026 Guide)" (https://www.digitalpolo.com/proportion-in-design/); Made Good Designs "Proportion in Graphic Design."

- HARD CEILING: 3 to 4 distinct hierarchy levels is the practical limit for most layouts. "More than that creates visual noise rather than structure." If you need more distinction, use weight, color, or spacing, do NOT add a fifth size.
- Baseline structure is heading / subheading / body. Everything else (caption, meta, pager) is optional and should be earned.
- POSTER-GRADE RATIOS (aggressive, for drama): Augmented Fourth 1.414, Perfect Fourth 1.333, Perfect Fifth 1.5, Golden Ratio 1.618. For "dramatic hierarchy, use ratios between 1.5 and 1.618." Editorial and poster work specifically want the more pronounced steps (1.333 and up) because the headline must dominate.
- A modular scale removes guesswork: pick one base size and one ratio, derive every other size from it, so all sizes are proportionally related. This is exactly what a systematized carousel should do.
- Rule of thumb repeated across sources: a strong typographic poster "communicates one idea with immediate clarity, with a single headline or phrase carrying 70%+ of visual weight."

### 5. One focal point + figure-ground contrast (the scroll-stopper) [BACKGROUND theory]

Sources: Smashing Magazine "Design Principles: Space And The Figure-Ground Relationship" (fetched in full, https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2014/05/design-principles-space-figure-ground-relationship/); Interaction Design Foundation "Law of Figure-Ground"; designyourway.net "Why is a Focal Point Essential."

- Figure and ground are mutually exclusive and complementary. Change one, you change the other. Three states: stable (one dominates, what we want), reversible (equal tension), ambiguous (avoid). A scroll-stopping slide needs a STABLE figure-ground: one clear figure, maximum contrast against ground.
- Jan Tschichold, quoted by Smashing: "White space is to be regarded as an active element, not a passive background."
- MICRO vs MACRO space: micro-space is inside groups (letter, word, line spacing); macro-space is between major blocks and guides eye movement plus provides rest. Both must be deliberate.
- Practical laws from the article: asymmetrical active space draws the eye more than passive symmetrical layouts; do not overfill; "people tolerate excess space better than insufficient space"; space correlates with perceived quality (luxury retail vs discount retail comparison).
- The one-focal-point rule (designyourway + IxDF): multiple elements competing to be the figure confuses the viewer and dilutes the message. Cleanest focal technique is ISOLATION: pull one element away from everything else, surround it with breathing room, the eye goes straight there.
- Eye-tracking backing: "Contrast and spatial positioning are the two strongest predictors of where fixation lands first." Color contrast is the fastest attention grabber. Carousel eye-tracking datasets (RecGaze, arXiv 2504.20792; "Riding the Carousel," arXiv 2507.10135) confirm strong top-down and left-right browsing bias plus a bias toward the first-presented item, which is why the cover slide and top-left of each slide are the prime real estate.

### 6. Gestalt grouping (how to structure the little text you keep) [BACKGROUND]

Sources: NN/G "Proximity Principle in Visual Design" (https://www.nngroup.com/articles/gestalt-proximity/); IxDF Gestalt topic; Superside "11 Gestalt Principles."

- PROXIMITY: elements close together read as one group; more whitespace between elements signals more separation. This is how you kill the "9 stacked reading zones" problem, group ruthlessly, and let the gaps do the work instead of rules and borders.
- COMMON REGION: objects inside the same closed region read as grouped, even without proximity. This is the theoretical justification for a single tint-block or bracket instead of a full bordered card. One region, not nine boxes.
- SIMILARITY: consistent color/shape/font ties related items; break similarity to signal "this one is different" (the accent).
- Chunking helps scanning. But the fewer chunks, the faster the scan. Under-15-words means ideally ONE chunk per slide.

### 7. Brutalist / editorial poster composition [BACKGROUND aesthetic, CURRENT 2026 revival]

Sources: Kittl "What is the Brutalist Design Art Style" (https://www.kittl.com/blogs/brutalist-design-art-stl/); designyourway.net "Brutalist Graphic Design"; Studio 2am "Raw by Design: Why Brutalist and Industrial Type Is Taking Over" (https://studio2am.co/blogs/news/raw-by-design-why-brutalist-and-industrial-type-is-taking-over); Zeka Design "Brutalism in Graphic Design."

- Type IS the image. Oversized type that dominates the page, or text used AS the grid itself. Heavy sans, all-caps, or default monospace/system fonts. This maps cleanly onto our technical-brutalist house style.
- Deliberate grid violation: establish an obvious grid, then break it once (bleed type off the edge, rotate one element, jam leading tight or blow it loose). The violation is the interest. Note: violate ONCE per composition, not everywhere, or it becomes noise again.
- Color: mostly black and white, and "when color shows up, it shows up loud." A single accent hue against an achromatic field (hot pink, electric yellow). One accent, high saturation.
- Keep imperfections. Scan artifacts, grain, overexposure left in on purpose read as "a human made this."

### 8. Swiss contrast theory: Ruder and Hofmann [BACKGROUND]

Sources: Encyclopedia of Design "Emil Ruder" (https://encyclopedia.design/2023/12/16/emil-ruder-a-pillar-of-swiss-typography-and-design/); Poster House "Armin Hofmann 1920-2020" (https://posterhouse.org/blog/armin-hofmann-1920-2020/).

- Ruder: study contrast at TWO levels. Macro = the overall distribution of black and white across the page. Micro = counters, letter/word/line spacing. The moment one letter hits the page, a network of relationships starts between the black form and the white ground, and between counterforms and surrounding space. So the empty parts of the slide are not leftover, they are half the composition.
- Hofmann: contrast was his number-one design element. His contrast pairs: light/dark, curved/straight, form/counterform, soft/hard, dynamic/static. Resolution is reached when all contrasting elements are "brought into absolute harmony." A slide should pick ONE dominant contrast axis and push it hard.

### 9. Massimo Vignelli's five-principle spine (for a house design language) [BACKGROUND]

Sources: Vignelli Canon (read directly) plus Creative Bloq "What Massimo Vignelli can teach designers today." Design begins with architecture (grid, proportion, hierarchy), not aesthetics. Fewer typefaces means deeper mastery. Hierarchy via scale gradations, avoid bold/italic when possible. Refuse trends so the work never ages. The Canon's intangibles: Semantics (meaning first, "the very first thing I do is search for the meaning"), Syntactics (consistency of the visual language, "syntactic consistency is of paramount importance"), Pragmatics (it must be understood or it fails), plus Discipline, Appropriateness, Ambiguity avoidance, Intellectual Elegance, Timelessness.

### 10. Reference designers to mine for scroll-stopping single-idea posters [BACKGROUND]

- Reid Miles, Blue Note Records (approx 500 covers, 1950s-60s). In the 1960s the album title itself became the focal point: very large type, tiny corner images, non-traditional crops, aggressive asymmetry and contrast. Source: arielsalminen.com "The Iconic Work of Reid Miles"; fontsinuse.com/designers/1906/reid-miles.
- Anthony Burrill: pared-down monochrome, historic wood/letterpress type, short powerful statements set in bold woodblock type ("Work Hard and Be Nice to People"). Source: anthonyburrill.com; itsnicethat.com feature (dated 2025-05-01).
- Experimental Jetset: Helvetica-driven typographic posters, text-as-image. Source: grafagallery.com, Pinterest reference boards.
These three are the visual north star for "one idea, huge type, near-zero body copy."

### 11. 4:5 portrait composition placement [BACKGROUND theory applied to CURRENT format]

Sources: ExpertPhotography "Golden Ratio Composition vs Rule of Thirds"; PetaPixel "Why The Golden Ratio Is Better Than The Rule Of Thirds"; Two Loves Studio "6 Composition Grids."

- Rule of thirds gives a symmetric 3x3; the phi grid (golden) is 1 : 1.618 : 1, so the middle band is narrower and the power points sit slightly closer to center, reading as more organic and less mechanical.
- Rule of thirds is best for minimal scenes with a single subject (exactly our case, one line of type per slide). The phi grid suits busier compositions. For our under-15-word slides, thirds is the correct, simpler tool.
- 4:5 at 1080x1350 gives about 35% more vertical screen space than a square, so vertical stacking of hierarchy (headline high, one line of support lower, everything else in generous air) is the natural move.

### 12. CURRENT (2026) carousel application: word counts, motion, algorithm

Sources: Slidy Creator "Instagram Carousel Trends" (fetched, https://slidycreator.com/blog/instagram-carousel-trends/); Scrolo "Top Trending Instagram Carousel Design Ideas 2026"; TryMyPost "Instagram Carousel Algorithm 2026"; Your Social Team "Hold and Scroll" (fetched); New Engen "Instagram Trends July 2026" (https://newengen.com/insights/instagram-trends/).

WORD COUNTS (this is the hard target): the 2026 standard is MICRO-LEARNING, 15 to 20 words maximum per slide with a strong visual anchor. Another source caps at "under 30 words" but says if a slide can't be absorbed in 2 to 3 seconds it is too dense. Treat 15 words as the ceiling, aim lower on the cover.

FORMAT: vertical 4:5 at 1080x1350 is the dominant performer. 7 to 10 slides is the engagement sweet spot.

FIRST SLIDE / COVER: the single most critical element. It is where the scroll stops. It must be a poster, one focal point, highly specific and emotionally resonant hook, not a generic promise. Slide 2 must immediately pay off the hook with no intro.

SEAMLESS / PANORAMIC: visual elements (lines, arrows, images) bleed from one slide onto the next; cutting a compelling visual off mid-slide forces the swipe. Reported ~40% higher completion vs standard.

MOTION / ANIMATED SLIDES (directly relevant to Carter's "animated slides" goal):
- Mixed-media carousels (photos + video clips) perform better, roughly 2.3% engagement, up to 20 items, video up to 60s each.
- "Hold and Scroll" / animated flipbook carousels: each slide has a SLIGHT visual change from the last, so swiping fast produces motion (flipbook). Mechanics that work: torn-paper reveal, text typed one word per slide, a landscape panning slide by slide. Cover slide carries the instruction "Hold the dots and scroll." Works because it breaks the scroll pattern, is interactive, and raises dwell time, which the algorithm rewards. No source gave a hard frame count; stop-motion logic (small delta per frame) is the method.
- Subtle motion on the first slide (cinemagraph, subtle zoom) reported to raise stop-scroll power by up to 22% vs a static header.

ALGORITHM: 2026 weights sends-per-reach (DM shares) and saves far above likes. Final slides should be reference/checklist material to earn the save. Carousels drive roughly 114% more engagement than single images.

TYPOGRAPHY TREND: sans-serif (Inter, Outfit) over scripts; bold type, clean layouts, high contrast, strategic whitespace. Note this trend list also pushes soft gradients and glassmorphism, which is OFF-brand for our technical-brutalist look and should be ignored; take the whitespace/contrast/bold-type parts, drop the gradient-glass parts.

## SO WHAT FOR US

Translate the above into build rules for a 1080x1350 slide with under 15 words. These are the changes to the make-carousel system.

RULE 1: TWO TYPE SIZES PER SLIDE, MAXIMUM. Per Vignelli's literal rule. One big (the headline/idea), one small (the single support line or label). If a slide currently has hud row + kicker + multi-line headline + paragraph + tag row + stat + pager + button, that is six-plus sizes and it violates the two-size law. Kill sizes; use scale contrast plus space for hierarchy.

RULE 2: THREE HIERARCHY LEVELS TOTAL, HARD CEILING (headline, support, and the persistent nav/brand). Never a fourth reading zone. If we need more distinction, reach for weight/color/space, not another font size. This directly attacks the "6 to 9 horizontal reading zones" problem.

RULE 3: ONE FOCAL POINT, ISOLATED. Each slide has exactly one figure. Give it maximum figure-ground contrast (our dark ground, one bright accent or white figure) and surround it with air so isolation does the pointing. No competing second hero.

RULE 4: WHITESPACE IS THE MAIN MATERIAL. Target a composition that is mostly empty with one dense cluster. Editorial theory: an 80% negative-space layout with one dense type cluster commands attention that a balanced layout never will. Adopt a fixed generous margin and a big vertical air gap between the headline block and everything else. "White space provides the silence."

RULE 5: A REAL MODULAR SCALE, NOT AD-HOC SIZES. Pick one base and one aggressive ratio (1.333 Perfect Fourth to 1.5 Perfect Fifth for editorial drama; do not exceed 1.618). Derive the headline clamp and the support clamp from it. This systematizes the "hero huge, support small" contrast and keeps every slide in the deck proportionally related. Note Carter's tighter-density memory: bias the clamps toward the smaller end of "poster" so it reads editorial/dossier at 0.8x zoom, not billboard, but keep the RATIO between the two sizes wide.

RULE 6: GROUP WITH SPACE AND ONE REGION, NOT NINE BORDERS. Use proximity and common-region: the few elements that belong together sit close in one quiet region; everything else is pushed apart by whitespace. This is the theoretical backing for Carter's "thin rules + whitespace, stop stacking bordered cards" memory. One structural-decoration moment per slide, the rest is text + air.

RULE 7: PICK ONE CONTRAST AXIS PER SLIDE AND PUSH IT (Hofmann). Big vs small, or light vs dark, or dense vs empty. Do not run all axes at once. Resolution = harmony of one dominant contrast, not a pile of them.

RULE 8: BREAK THE GRID EXACTLY ONCE (brutalist). Establish the grid, then one deliberate violation per slide max: bleed the number off the edge, oversize one glyph, rotate one label. More than one violation and it becomes the busy noise we are escaping.

RULE 9: THE COVER IS A POSTER, NOT A SLIDE. Fewest words of the whole deck, one focal point, specific emotional hook, and it is the make-or-break for the scroll stop (eye-tracking bias to first item + top-left). Reid Miles / Burrill / Experimental Jetset are the references: title as the image, huge type, tiny or no support.

RULE 10: BUILD MOTION AS A NEW CAPABILITY. Carter's pipeline renders standalone HTML per slide via shotpls. To ship animated slides we need a new render path: render N incremental frames of one slide (small delta per frame, flipbook logic) to an MP4 the way we render PNGs, then post as a mixed-media carousel item. Highest-ROI first motion features, in order: (a) subtle zoom/cinemagraph on the cover only (reported +22% stop-scroll), (b) "hold and scroll" seamless panorama where a line/arrow bleeds across slides, (c) one-word-per-frame typed headline. Keep it monochrome + one accent to stay on our brutalist brand; ignore the gradient/glassmorphism trend.

RULE 11: LAST SLIDE EARNS THE SAVE. Algorithm weights saves and DM-shares over likes. Make the final slide a compact reference/checklist so people save it, and keep a specific comment-keyword CTA for the DM funnel.

RULE 12: TIMELESSNESS OVER TREND. Vignelli: refusing trends is why work never ages. Bank the durable moves (grid, two sizes, whitespace, one focal point, one contrast axis). Treat the 2026 trend layer (motion, seamless) as capability to add, not as a look to chase, and hard-skip the gradient/glass fashion.

BOTTOM-LINE MANDATE FOR THE REDESIGN: fewer sizes, fewer zones, more air, one focal point, one contrast axis, one grid-break. Vignelli already wrote our indictment: "Bad layouts have no space left for breathing, every little space covered by a cacophony of type-sizes, images, and screaming titles." That is the current deck. The fix is silence.

## ADDENDUM (second research pass, 2026-07-10): worked examples, the dominance ceiling, and validator tooling

This pass adds material the first pass did not cover: a canonical dominance rule, two fully-analyzed reference posters (one figure-ground, one pure-geometry), concrete headline-to-body point pairs, and a proposal to ENFORCE the poster in the pipeline rather than just permit it. Additional searches run this pass: "Josef Muller-Brockmann grid systems one focal point typographic hierarchy"; "typographic scale ratio how many levels maximum poster"; "figure ground contrast focal point poster scroll stopping visual weight"; "editorial magazine layout one big word poster type contrast"; "Armin Hofmann Giselle poster 1959 analysis figure ground"; "Muller-Brockmann Beethoven Tonhalle poster concentric geometry reduction"; "poster rule of thirds asymmetric balance one word type dominant"; "one idea per slide carousel dwell time hook first slide contrast thumbnail". Pages fetched: Smashing "Dominance, Focal Points and Hierarchy"; madegooddesigns poster principles; Scrolo 2026 trends.

A1. THE DOMINANCE CEILING (Smashing, "Design Principles: Dominance, Focal Points And Hierarchy," https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2015/02/design-principles-dominance-focal-points-hierarchy/). Exact rules: "You can't emphasize everything. In order for some elements in a design to stand out, other elements must fade into the background." "Identical items can't dominate each other. To exert dominance, an element has to look different from the elements it's meant to dominate." Hierarchy ceiling stated flatly: "Three levels is ideal; they're all that most people can discern," and "Unless you're sure you can contrast each level well enough, stick with three." Three tiers named: DOMINANT (single entry point, max weight) / SUB-DOMINANT (secondary focal) / SUBORDINATE (body, fine print). Design for "distinct levels, not a continuum." This is independent corroboration of the first pass's Vignelli two-size rule and our three-level ceiling: our current 6-9 zones are a continuum, which is the failure state both sources name.

A2. WORKED EXAMPLE, FIGURE-GROUND: Armin Hofmann, Giselle, 1959 (90 x 128 cm photomontage). Sources: SFMOMA https://www.sfmoma.org/artwork/2014.669/ ; MoMA https://www.moma.org/collection/works/6775 ; Cooper Hewitt https://www.cooperhewitt.org/2018/08/05/aharmonyofcontrasts/ . The entire poster is two elements: the word "Giselle" set LARGE and VERTICAL down the left, and one blurred white cropped photo of a ballerina mid-spin on the right. Figure-ground IS the mechanism: "the organic, soft body of the ballet dancer in motion contrasts with the stationary, sharply geometric typographic forms"; "the bright white of the lettering contrasts with the grey tones of the photograph." The dancer is "cut off at the head and foot," so cropping turns a photo into a graphic shape. Monochrome. And the whole thing "pivots graphically on the dot on the letter i" (one tiny accent). Lesson for a portrait slide: one huge word + one cropped high-contrast shape + one micro accent = a complete poster.

A3. WORKED EXAMPLE, PURE GEOMETRY: Josef Muller-Brockmann, Beethoven / Zurich Tonhalle, 1955. Geometric analysis from Kimberly Elam, "Geometry of Design" (Princeton Architectural Press), reproduced at https://www.behance.net/gallery/9862277/Mueller-Brockmanns-Beethoven-Poster-Geometric-Analysis ; context at https://musubi.black/thought-leadership/josef-muller-brockmann . Stark black/white concentric arcs, ZERO body copy. Every measurement is systematized: arc rotation on "a module of 11.25 degrees"; arc lengths run 5, 6, 7 segments; arc WIDTHS follow a doubling series 1, 2, 4, 8, 16. The geometry maps to the subject ("geometric rhythms of the concentric arcs relate directly to the mathematical systems present in the composer's music"). Lesson: one modular geometric system, driven by a single unit + a doubling scale, is enough to carry a slide with only a label. This is a concrete build target for text-free slides and, because it is parametric, it is trivially ANIMATABLE (rings drawing on, bars doubling) for the motion path in Rule 10.

A4. CONCRETE HEADLINE-TO-BODY POINT PAIRS. Two specific pairs recur across poster/editorial sources and give us a target ratio. Poster: "a headline set at 150 points against body text at 24 points creates an unmistakable reading order" (~6:1). Source: madegooddesigns https://madegooddesigns.com/poster-design-principles/ and the rule-of-thirds/composition results. Editorial: "a 64px headline next to 16px body copy creates its own grid" (4:1). Source: editorial hierarchy guides. Both say the same thing: the JUMP between the hero and the caption must be unmistakable (4:1 to 6:1), with essentially nothing in between. This is the numeric backbone for the modular scale in the first pass's Rule 5, and it reconciles with Carter's tighter-density memory by keeping the RATIO wide (6:1) while allowing the absolute sizes to sit at the editorial, not billboard, end.

A5. ASYMMETRIC SPLIT + POSTER CONSTRAINTS (madegooddesigns poster principles, corroborating first pass): allocate roughly 1/3 to imagery and 2/3 to text or the reverse, never 1/2 : 1/2. Limit to "two or three typefaces at most" and "two or three colors at most." Whole-poster text ceiling "50-75 words" (a single slide is a fraction of that). Contrast: "Black text on a white background or white text on a dark background will always outperform subtle color combinations." Legibility standard: a poster headline "should be legible from at least three to five meters," tested by the SQUINT test ("squint at the poster from across a room").

A6. 50-MILLISECOND SCROLL-STOP + HOOK SPEC (Pano, https://panocollages.com/blog/best-practices-for-first-slide-carousel-hooks , 2026). The cover pattern interrupt must "stop the scroll in under 50 milliseconds" via high-contrast color, an expressive face, or a striking graphic. Hook headline = 5-8 specific words with a curiosity gap; specific numbers/outcomes beat generic. Add a directional cue extending toward the RIGHT edge to invite the first swipe. Minimum text contrast 4.5:1 (WCAG). This puts a hard latency budget on the cover: if the figure-ground does not resolve in a blink, it fails.

A7. NEW TOOLING TO ENFORCE THE POSTER (build these; Carter has full pipeline freedom, terminal + shotpls). The theory only helps if the render pipeline REJECTS non-posters:
- Squint/blur validator: after shotpls renders each 2160x2700 PNG, auto-generate a heavily Gaussian-blurred copy (Pillow via uv, or an ImageMagick one-liner in the Justfile). If ONE element does not still visibly dominate the blur, the slide failed the poster test. This operationalizes the squint test (A5) and the dominance ceiling (A1).
- Word-count linter per slide HTML: fail the render if visible text exceeds ~15 words (first pass Rule 1 / 2026 micro-learning standard).
- Type-size linter: parse the slide's computed font sizes; fail if more than 3 distinct sizes appear (enforces Vignelli two-size + Smashing three-level ceiling), or if the headline-to-support ratio is under ~4:1 (enforces A4).
- Contrast checker: compute WCAG contrast of each text run against its background; fail under 4.5:1 (A6).
