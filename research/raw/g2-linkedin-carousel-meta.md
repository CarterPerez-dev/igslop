## FINDINGS

### 1. Is LinkedIn document reach rising or falling? Answer: platform-wide reach fell hard through 2023-2025, but has STABILIZED in 2026, and documents remain the single strongest FORMAT. The nuance: infographics are overtaking multi-slide carousels at the very top.

Ground-truth dataset. Saywhat State of the Algorithm Q1 2026 report, based on 397,605 posts (co-founder Will McTighe). Reported at saywhat.ai/algorithm-webinar and growth.chris-donnelly.co.uk/q1-2026-algo-report-page:
- Median impressions are down 68% from the Q3 2023 peak, but down just 2% between Q4 2025 and Q1 2026. The decline has largely stabilized. So "reach is falling" is a 2023-2024 story, not a 2026 story. In 2026 it is roughly flat.
- The LinkedIn algorithm changed in March 2026: relevance now beats reach. Niche expertise gets rewarded over generic broad appeal. (Directly relevant to Carter: a cybersecurity/cert niche is exactly what this rewards.)
- Hashtags: more than 3 hashtags performed 71% worse than posts with none.
- Video usage surged 69% YoY but video reach declined 35-72% due to low completion. Exception: short vertical video (30-90s) with captions saw reach increase 80%; horizontal video reach declined 18%.

Broader platform decline numbers (widely repeated across dataslayer.ai, yepads.com, carouselli.com, tracing back to van der Blom): views down 50%, engagement down 25%, follower growth down 59% versus last year. Treat these as the multi-year contraction, which Saywhat says has now flattened.

The carousel-vs-infographic shift at the top (the important 2026 wrinkle):
- Saywhat Q1 2026: infographics are now the single most common format among top 1% posts. Reported as 28.6% by dataslayer.ai and as 27.4% of outliers by Kara Redman's synthesis (same report, minor discrepancy, both ~28%). Multi-slide carousel reach is declining quarter on quarter at the very top while infographics rise.
- Both carousels and infographics still hit roughly 3x the reach of text-only posts. The takeaway is NOT abandon carousels, it is that a single strong visual (a "poster" / one-frame infographic) is now the strongest visual play on LinkedIn. This is the same conclusion Carter's own Instagram data pushes toward.
- Saywhat (per Kara Redman): carousels had the highest save rate of any format at 29.2%; carousels reached 4.1x text posts; infographics 3.4x text posts.

### 2. Documents are still the top-performing FORMAT by engagement (multiple independent datasets agree on ~6.6%).

- Socialinsider (1M posts, via Kara Redman + dataslayer): document posts 6.6% engagement rate, the highest of any format. Average dwell time on a document 15-20 seconds.
- Dataslayer's own May 2026 numbers: documents 6.60% engagement (highest), native video 5.60%, image+text 3.20%, text-only 2.00%. Posts with external links get ~60% less reach.
- Richard van der Blom Algorithm Insights (600K+ posts, per Kara Redman): documents/carousels +6.92% reach and +46.73% engagement versus baseline. Text+image is called "the safest consistent format."
- AuthoredUp (3M+ posts): video declined most steeply, fell 72%.
- Polls collapsed after the March 2026 Authenticity Update: 0.07% engagement, effectively dead (dataslayer).

Caveat on the engagement-rate numbers: usevisuals.com reports much lower absolute rates (carousels 1.92%, video 1.2%, text 1.4%) but the same RELATIVE ordering (carousels 3.4x reach and 2.1x engagement vs single-image). Absolute engagement percentages vary wildly by how each firm computes the denominator; trust the ratios, not the raw percentages.

### 3. Saves are the currency, and this is why the poster format wins on LinkedIn too.

- Van der Blom (widely quoted): one save drives ~5x more reach than a like and ~2x more than a comment. One external link in the post body reduces median reach by 18.8%.
- carouselli / grokipedia framing: on LinkedIn a "Save" is a massive authority signal telling the algorithm the content is high-value reference material. Educational carousels get saved far more than text posts.
- usevisuals target: aim for a save-to-impression ratio of at least 0.5%.
- This is the exact same mechanism as Carter's Instagram save wins. Reference-value, saveable design is rewarded identically on both platforms. His image-artifact decks that pulled 2,700-6,600 saves are precisely the LinkedIn-optimal object too.

### 4. Design rules for LinkedIn PDF carousels (the ones that survive a cross-post).

Dimensions and file (oktopost, trymypost, postnitro):
- Portrait 4:5 at 1080x1350 px is the recommended/strongest size and is IDENTICAL to Carter's Instagram canvas. Square 1080x1080 is the alternative. This means his exact render target already satisfies LinkedIn. Zero re-layout needed on dimensions.
- PDF only for reliable cross-device rendering (PPTX/DOCX accepted but render inconsistently). Max 100 MB, up to 300 pages allowed (irrelevant, nobody uses that). Export as "PDF Standard" not "PDF Print": Print embeds CMYK and bloats the file 4-5x with no on-screen benefit (trymypost). 300 DPI is fine but not required for screen.
- Safe margin at least 50 px from all edges (oktopost).

Typography and density (converging across trymypost, oktopost, morphica, carouselli, linkboost, grokipedia):
- Minimum font size 24px within the 1080x1350 canvas; text under ~20-24px "becomes illegible when scrolling on a smartphone." Headlines 40px+.
- Word count per slide: the consensus is a hard ceiling. Sources say 20-30 words max, "no more than 30 words per slide," "under 50 words," "billboards not textbook pages." Morphica goes furthest: maximum two text blocks per slide (a headline plus one supporting element), 3-8 words per headline, body copy max two sentences, and explicitly "headline-only slides with no body text at all" as a viable strategy.
- carouselli's most-saved example: "a simple dark background, one large number per slide, and minimal copy. Restraint amplifies the data." One checklist item per slide with a large checkbox visual. Their measured lift: checklist format hit a 1.3% save rate, 3x the average.
- High-contrast text carousels perform 40% better than carousels with complex backgrounds (carouselli, usevisuals). Minimalist designs get a 12% higher completion rate (usevisuals).
- 2026 dark-mode palette for tech LinkedIn carousels (grokipedia): background #121212 (not pure #000000) as the dominant ~60% of the color distribution, neutral-first, one accent. This maps cleanly onto Carter's dark technical-brutalist Synthetic style.

Cover slide (oktopost, postnitro, socialchamp):
- One bold specific claim or data point, a clear topic statement, minimal visual clutter, one headline on a contrasting background, and a visible "swipe" indicator. Stop the scroll in under 2 seconds.

Slide count (the one real divergence from Instagram):
- LinkedIn sweet spot lands at 6-10 (morphica, oktopost, postnitro, trymypost), with several sources stretching the practitioner range to 8-15 (brandghost) because LinkedIn users read more deeply. Fewer than 5 "feels incomplete." Beyond 12-15, completion rates drop ~40%.
- Each swipe adds dwell time, LinkedIn's strongest ranking signal. A 10-slide carousel holds attention 15-20s (some say 30-60s) versus 3-5s for a skimmed text post. More (well-designed) slides literally buys ranking on LinkedIn in a way it does not on Instagram.

### 5. How top creators design DIFFERENTLY for LinkedIn vs Instagram (the cross-post delta).

From postnitro.ai (direct LinkedIn vs Instagram comparison) and pineable/socialchamp:
- Instagram: viewers scroll fast, decide in a split second, stop for strong visuals. "One point per slide," large type, strong contrast, short lines, 5-7 slides to avoid swipe fatigue. Imagery can carry a slide.
- LinkedIn: viewers show up to READ and learn. Text-forward, step-by-step, substance-driven, analytical. 6-10+ slides. "Lead with ideas, not decoration." Data and examples are welcome. Imagery supports, it does not carry.
- What TRANSFERS unchanged: the core idea, brand colors, professional quality, and a strong opening thesis/hook. The 1080x1350 canvas transfers.
- What must CHANGE: slide depth/complexity (add slides for LinkedIn), caption formality (LinkedIn caption is longer and more analytical), visual density (LinkedIn tolerates a bit more), and CTA softness. Instagram CTA = "save/share." LinkedIn CTA = ask a question to spark comments. Direct copy-paste cross-posting "rarely works," reframe per audience.
- Named creators built on carousels (buffer, meet-lea, tryunibox): Justin Welsh ("The Diversified Solopreneur," popularized the how-to-guide carousel), Lara Acosta, Matt Barker, Sahil Bloom. Their decks are recognizable from the thumbnail alone (consistent template/branding). Justin Welsh examples cited: "7 Steps to Build a Personal Brand" 500,000+ impressions; "5-Step Framework for Client Acquisition" 2,000+ shares; "8 Habits of Successful Solopreneurs" 3,000+ comments. Justin Welsh's own 2026 guide page (justinwelsh.me/article/linkedin-guide-2026) is a link index and did not expose full article text to fetch, so I could not extract his exact per-slide rules directly, only the secondhand summaries above.

### 6. HARD CONSTRAINT: animated/video slides do NOT survive into a LinkedIn PDF.

- LinkedIn PDF carousels are static documents. Any animated element renders as a static image (connectsafely, trymypost). Uploaded GIFs are converted to a static first frame unless posted as a native standalone GIF under 5MB / 400 frames, which does not apply inside a document.
- To get motion on LinkedIn you must use the native video upload, which sacrifices the swipe/document mechanic entirely (you lose the save-heavy document format and its 6.6% engagement).
- Implication for Carter: any animated or video slide he builds for the Instagram carousel is DEAD on the LinkedIn PDF. The LinkedIn export must use a static "poster" version of that slide (a strong still frame or the same info as a static infographic). Design the deck so every slide reads perfectly as a static frame first; treat animation as an Instagram-only enhancement layer, never load-bearing.

## SO WHAT FOR US

1. The poster pivot is the RIGHT call on both platforms, not just Instagram. LinkedIn's 2026 top-of-feed moved from multi-slide carousels to single-frame infographics (~28% of top 1% posts). "One large number per slide, dark background, minimal copy" is the literal most-saved LinkedIn pattern and it is the same thing Carter's 47k-65k-view / 2,700-6,600-save Instagram decks already do. Poster design is not an Instagram-only bet, it is where LinkedIn is going too.

2. His canvas is already correct. 1080x1350 4:5 is the recommended LinkedIn size, identical to his Instagram render. No re-layout for dimensions. The shotpls 2160x2700 PNG downsizes cleanly, and the PDF should be assembled from the SAME slide HTMLs. Build one deck, export two outputs.

3. Add a static-first rule to the make-carousel skill, enforced by the cross-post. Because LinkedIn PDFs are strictly static and kill all animation, every slide must fully deliver its point as a still frame. Animation/video slides become an Instagram-only enhancement layer over a static base, never the base itself. Practically: build the static slide first, render it to PNG for both the IG static path AND the LinkedIn PDF, then optionally animate a copy for IG only.

4. Two output profiles from one build, differing on slide COUNT and CAPTION, not design. Instagram: cut to the tightest 5-8 slides, punchiest posters, CTA = "save this / comment KEYWORD." LinkedIn: keep or extend to 8-12 slides (extra swipes = more dwell = more reach there), a longer analytical caption, CTA = an open question to pull comments, and zero-to-one hashtag (3+ hashtags cost 71% reach). Same visual system, two assemblies.

5. Design constraints to bake into the skill (both platforms, they do not conflict): minimum 24px type in the 1080x1350 canvas, 40px+ headlines, hard cap ~30 words per slide (ideally one headline + one supporting line, headline-only slides encouraged), high contrast on a dark #121212-ish background with one accent (his Synthetic style already), one focal point per slide, a visible swipe cue on the cover, and 50px safe margins. High-contrast text beat complex backgrounds by 40%, minimalism raised completion 12%. This is a data-backed license to strip the "6-9 reading zones per slide" busyness.

6. Optimize for the SAVE, explicitly. A save is worth ~5x a like on LinkedIn and drives Instagram reach the same way. Every deck should be built as saveable reference material (a number, a checklist item, a cheat-sheet line per slide). Carter's cert/tool/salary content is natively reference-shaped, lean into it. Aim for the 0.5%+ save-to-impression floor.

7. Consider building a dedicated single-frame "infographic poster" format alongside the carousel. Since ~28% of LinkedIn's top 1% is now single infographics and both formats beat text 3x, a one-slide poster (his strongest single frame, standalone) is a low-cost, high-ceiling LinkedIn play his HTML pipeline can generate trivially. Test single-poster posts against multi-slide decks on LinkedIn.

8. Niche is a tailwind now. The March 2026 relevance-over-reach shift rewards narrow expertise. His cybersecurity/certification niche is exactly what LinkedIn's 2026 algorithm favors, so consistent topical decks compound topic authority rather than getting diluted.
