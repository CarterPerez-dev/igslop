1. THE FORMAT-LEVEL VERDICT: CAROUSELS WIN ON THE METRIC WE CARE ABOUT (SAVES)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Two independent large-N 2026 studies agree on the core hierarchy: carousels beat Reels
and single images on ENGAGEMENT-PER-REACH and on SAVES specifically, while Reels win on
raw discovery reach. For an education/authority account whose funnel is save-then-DM, the
carousel is the correct primary format.

PRIMARY STUDY A: Buffer, "State of Social Media Engagement 2026," 52M+ posts across 10
platforms. Engagement rate BY REACH (likes+comments+saves+shares / reach):
- Carousels: 6.90 percent
- Single images: 4.44 percent
- Reels: 3.31 percent
- Carousels earn roughly 109 percent more engagement per person reached than Reels.
- Buffer framing: Reels = discovery mode (reach non-followers), carousels = relationship
  mode (deeper interaction from existing followers). Reels get ~36 percent more reach than
  carousels in Buffer's data.
Source: buffer.com/resources/state-of-social-media-engagement-2026/

PRIMARY STUDY B: Socialinsider 2026 Instagram benchmarks. Sample: 35M posts from 447,613
pages, Jan-Dec 2025 (published early 2026, quarterly updates). Overall engagement rate by
format:
- Carousels: 0.55 percent
- Reels: 0.52 percent
- Images: 0.37 percent
Note: Socialinsider's absolute numbers are much lower than Buffer's because Socialinsider
divides by FOLLOWERS, Buffer divides by REACH. Different denominators, same ranking.

SOCIALINSIDER SAVES BY FOLLOWER TIER (median saves per post) - this is the most useful table
for us because our metric is saves and we are a small-to-mid account:
  Followers   Reels  Carousels  Images
  1-5K          1        1        1
  5-10K         2        2        1
  10-50K        7        8        3
  50-100K      22       35       10
  100K-1M      96       98       43
Read: at the 10-50K and 50-100K tiers carousels out-save Reels, and the gap WIDENS with size
(50-100K: carousels 35 vs Reels 22 saves). Carousels are a save machine as the account grows.

SOCIALINSIDER VIEWS BY FOLLOWER TIER (median): carousels actually beat Reels on views in this
dataset at every tier (e.g. 10-50K: carousels 4,275 vs Reels 2,460; 50-100K: 11,597 vs 6,095).
This contradicts the common "Reels get more reach" claim and shows the reach story is dataset
dependent. Treat "Reels reach more" as true for cold non-follower discovery, not as universal.

SECONDARY / RE-QUOTED (treat as directional, not gospel):
- Multiple blogs cite "carousels 1.92 percent vs Reels 0.50 vs images 0.45." This 1.92 figure
  is not traceable to a named primary study in what I read; it is a blog-circulated number.
- Blogs also cite "carousels 10.15 percent engagement" (attributed to TrueFuture Media Jan 2026)
  and "carousels 6.90 percent" (Buffer). The 6.90 is the real Buffer number; the 10.15 is a
  blog aggregate. Do not put 10 percent in our own copy as fact.
- "Carousels get 1.4x more reach than static posts" is attributed to Adam Mosseri and appears
  across many sources; Mosseri's actual statement is softer: more media means more interactions
  means more reach on average. The 1.4x is a blog quantification, not a Mosseri quote.
- Mixed-media (image+video) carousels: 2.33 percent vs 1.80 percent image-only (postnitro,
  March 27 2026). Roughly a 29 percent lift claimed. Blog-level, single source, but the
  DIRECTION (video slides help) is corroborated by contentdrips and creatorflow.

2. IDEAL SLIDE COUNT: THE HONEST RANGE IS 6-10, WITH A HARD FLOOR AT 4-5
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Sources DISAGREE on the exact optimum, so here is the real spread rather than a fake single
number:
- carouselli "12 rules": 5-8 slides. Below 4 lacks content, above 10 measurably reduces
  completion.
- marketingagent.blog: optimal 6-13 slides; recommends by type (before/after 8-10, tutorial
  8-10, myth-busting 9-11, data/stats 9-10, interactive 10).
- contentdrips / creatorflow / multiple: "8-10 slides is the current sweet spot; under 5
  underperforms."
- truefuturemedia + bestcontent.ai: 8-12 for educational, 12-20 only for deep guides,
  photo-dump storytelling, or case studies where extra context earns saves.
- trymypost Q1 2026 claim: posts with 5-7 slides generate 3.4x more saves and 2.1x more shares
  than static images; posts over 12 slides see a 40 percent drop-off in completion UNLESS
  content is highly serialized storytelling.

Synthesis for us: 7 to 9 slides is the defensible sweet spot for an educational tech deck.
Never ship under 5. Only exceed 10 when the content is genuinely serialized (a numbered
cheat sheet where the list itself pulls people through). More slides is not free; every extra
slide past the value payload lowers completion, and completion is a distribution signal.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE COMPLETION-RATE MECHANIC (why the busy-slide problem actually costs reach)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is the single most actionable mechanic for Carter's "too busy" problem.

- Instagram tracks swipe-through rate / completion rate (percent who reach the last slide).
  High swipe-through carousels get distributed to 3-5x more non-followers than low-swipe ones.
  (Attributed to Meta internal data via carouselli and other blogs; treat the 3-5x as
  directional, but the mechanism is well established.)
- The slide 1 -> slide 2 transition is the single most critical friction point. If the user
  does not swipe immediately, the algorithm assumes disinterest. (marketingagent.blog)
- Slides 1-3 determine swipe-through rate. Engagement typically dips after slide 3 and recovers
  at slide 8+ (the payoff/summary zone). Front-load value into slides 1-3.
- Panorama completion curve (postplanify, blog data, treat as directional): 2 slides ~90pct,
  3 slides ~80pct, 4 slides ~70pct, 5 slides ~60pct, 7-10 slides ~40pct. Every added slide
  costs completion. This is why 3-slide seamless panoramas are called the sweet spot.
- The RESURFACING mechanic (confirmed direction, multiple sources incl Mosseri logic): if a
  viewer does not finish, Instagram re-serves the carousel later STARTING FROM THE FIRST UNSEEN
  SLIDE, treating unseen slides as new content. A carousel is multiple shots on goal from one
  post. Implication: every slide should be able to function as an entry point / cold open.

WHY BUSY HURTS: dwell time and swipe-velocity are 2026 ranking signals. A slide with 6-9
reading zones raises cognitive load, slows the swipe, and if it stalls the reader it reads to
the algorithm as a drop-off. A poster slide with one focal point is swiped through fast and
CLEAN, which is a positive velocity signal, and it is legible in the 1-2 milliseconds people
give a slide while scanning. Clarity over complexity is repeatedly named as the 2026 rule.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. TEXT DENSITY PER SLIDE: THE POSTER STANDARD IS NOW EXPLICIT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The consensus for 2026 is aggressively minimal, which directly validates the poster pivot:
- "Micro-learning: 15-20 words MAXIMUM per slide, paired with a strong visual anchor." Use
  diagrams, charts, before/after visuals instead of text-heavy paragraphs. (scrolo, multiple)
- "One idea per slide. One point or one sentence maximum." (carouselli rule 4)
- Cover text under 8-10 words; "one idea, not three." (truefuturemedia, contentdrips)
- Cover hook headline 5-8 words, largest text on the slide. (multiple)
- "20 percent text rule": no single slide should exceed 20 percent text overlay; text is a
  caption/guide, the visual carries the slide. (marketingagent.blog)
- Body text minimum 24pt; paragraphs 1-2 sentences per slide max. (adaptlypost via search)

This is the direct instruction to kill the stacked-newspaper layout. The current 6-9 reading
zones per slide is roughly 3-5x the density the platform now rewards. Target: one headline,
one visual anchor, near-zero body copy per slide.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. COVER CONVENTIONS: THE "INCOMPLETE" COVER IS THE WINNING PATTERN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The cover carries ~80 percent of the weight (widely repeated). Winning conventions in 2026:
- Cover must answer instantly: "Is this for me?" and "What do I get if I swipe?"
- HOOK not TITLE. A title closes the loop ("5 Instagram tips"); a hook opens it ("The one
  mistake killing your reach"). Open loop = must-swipe. (carouselli rule 1, marketingagent)
- The "feel incomplete" move that works: cropped diagram, partial checklist, blurred result,
  split-screen before/after, or a big "#1 of 7". The cover should feel unfinished without a
  swipe. (truefuturemedia, bestcontent.ai) This is a design lever, not just a copy lever, and
  it maps cleanly to a poster cover.
- Cover formula (contentdrips): bold hook headline (5-8 words, largest element) + visual
  pattern interrupt (high-contrast color, expressive face, dramatic graphic) + curiosity
  trigger (partial reveal / surprising stat / open question).
- Proven cover templates named repeatedly: "Stop doing X," "3 signs," "Checklist,"
  "Mistakes," "Before/After," "Myth vs fact," "Template," "Costs / how much X really costs."
- Standalone test: slide 1 must stop the scroll on its own before you post. (carouselli rule 12)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. AESTHETIC: DESIGNED-POSTER vs PHOTO-DUMP vs HYBRID
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Both aesthetics win in 2026 but for different jobs; the trend is toward LESS polish overall,
and toward one clear focal point per slide regardless of style.

DESIGNED / POSTER / BOLD-TYPOGRAPHY (this is our lane):
- "Text-forward designs with large headlines and high-contrast fonts are performing extremely
  well because they communicate value instantly." (scrolo)
- Minimal aesthetic (neutral colors, simple fonts, ample spacing) reads as clean, modern,
  premium and is dominant in 2026.
- Editorial / magazine-inspired layouts signal premium authority. This is closest to a
  refined version of what Carter already does, minus the clutter.
- Trend note: soft premium aesthetics, subtle gradients, glassmorphism, modern typography are
  in; overly stylized script fonts are out. (search-level, multiple)

PHOTO-DUMP / SCRAPBOOK:
- Curated-casual photo dumps are one of the most-engaged formats, driving high saves AND the
  most comments/shares because they feel authentic and relatable. Gen Z favors polaroid frames,
  tape, handwritten fonts, doodles. (scrolo, inmagic, truefuturemedia)
- Key qualifier repeated everywhere: cohesion. Same filter / tones / lighting across the dump
  or it looks sloppy instead of intentional. Photo-dump is not "no design," it is "invisible
  design via consistency."
- For a technical authority account, pure photo-dump is off-brand, but the AUTHENTICITY signal
  is worth stealing.

HYBRID (the strategically interesting one for us):
- Designed poster cover + more raw/authentic interior slides (real terminal screenshots, real
  screen recordings, real artifacts) is the practical fusion. It keeps the scroll-stopping
  designed hook while the interior earns trust with real, un-glossy proof.
- "Authenticity over polish" is an explicit 2026 platform trend (Hootsuite, April 2026):
  selfie-cam / raw content is being algorithmically favored over over-produced studio content.
  Directionally this says our carousels can afford, and may benefit from, rawer interior slides
  (screenshots, real tool output) rather than everything being a perfectly-styled card.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. SEAMLESS / PANORAMA CONTINUOUS CAROUSELS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A high-performing 2026 format and one Carter's HTML-per-slide pipeline can produce natively
(render one wide artwork, slice into 1080-wide tiles).
- Sweet spot: 3 slides (~80 percent completion). 2 slides for a before/after reveal (~90pct).
- Technical: source width >= 1080px x slide count. 3-slide panorama needs a >=3240px-wide
  source; output each tile at 1080x1350 (4:5).
- Rules: put the striking element in slide 1; NEVER place faces or critical text on a split
  line (the seam falls between slides and cuts the element); keep filters/color identical
  across tiles; upload tiles in exact order (out-of-order upload is the #1 mistake and destroys
  the effect).
- Claimed lift: seamless carousels achieve ~40 percent higher completion rate than standard
  posts (2026, blog-level, directional). The "visual bleed from slide to slide creates a
  psychological urge to swipe" is the mechanism, and it directly counters our busy-slide
  problem because a panorama is by definition one focal composition, not stacked zones.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. ANIMATED / VIDEO SLIDES INSIDE THE CAROUSEL (a real 2026 innovation)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Carousels support mixed images AND video (up to 20 mixed items). This is the "animated slides"
the brief asks about, and it is a genuine edge.
- Lift: mixed image+video carousels ~2.33 percent vs ~1.80 percent image-only (postnitro,
  March 2026; single-source but corroborated in direction). contentdrips also reports mixed
  outperforms image-only.
- Placement patterns (postnitro):
  1. Opening hook (slides 1-2): motion as a pattern interrupt to stop the scroll.
  2. Mid-carousel (slides 3-7): video as the payoff / demo / credibility after a static
     problem setup.
  3. Closing (final slide): motion reinforcing the CTA.
- Video slide specs: up to 60s per slide, 1:1 or 4:5, MP4 H.264, 1080p, ~3-6 Mbps.
- Adding native music/audio pushes the carousel toward the Reels feed algorithm for extra
  reach while keeping carousel save-depth. (creatorflow; corroborated by creator Ned Potter
  who calls native music "absolutely crucial... it pushes the post into a different algorithm.")
- FRAME-BY-FRAME ANIMATION technique (scrolo, marketingagent): design sequential frames that
  "play out" as the user swipes, to demo a process, a transformation, or a reveal. This is a
  static-slide trick that FEELS animated with zero video, and it is directly buildable in the
  HTML-per-slide pipeline (render N frames of one evolving diagram/terminal state). Strong fit.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. VERIFIED 2026 PLATFORM UPDATES (build these into tooling assumptions)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

- 20-slide carousels: max raised from 10 to 20 (rolled out Aug 2024, still current). Supports
  mixed images + videos. (storrito, multiple)
- 3:4 aspect ratio (1080x1440) now OFFICIALLY supported by Instagram, confirmed via Mosseri
  (matches the 3:4 phone-camera default and the 3:4 profile-grid crop). Gives ~7 percent more
  vertical space than 4:5. IMPORTANT CAVEAT: the uploader still DEFAULTS to 4:5 (1080x1350),
  and 4:5 remains the recommended default with the longest engagement track record. Do NOT
  treat 1080x1440 as the new required size. One aggregator blog (techvint) framed 1080x1440 as
  a sweeping mandatory "new format" with zero official sourcing; that framing is unverified
  hype. Keep rendering 1080x1350 as the baseline; 3:4 is an option, not a requirement.
- Multiple captions per carousel slide: OFFICIAL, rolled out worldwide starting June 18, 2026.
  Each of up to 20 slides can carry its own caption. Instagram states the per-slide keywords
  ALSO improve search discoverability (each slide can target different keywords). This is a
  real, dateable, 2026 SEO lever for education content: put slide-specific searchable terms on
  every slide's caption (e.g. a different tool/CVE/cert term per slide). (Fast Company, Metricool,
  Campaign ME, Business Upturn, June 2026)

================================================================================
SO WHAT FOR US
================================================================================

1. THE POSTER PIVOT IS CORRECT AND PLATFORM-BACKED. The 2026 standard is 15-20 words MAX per
   slide, one idea per slide, 20 percent max text overlay, one visual anchor. Our current
   6-9 reading zones per slide is roughly 3-5x over the rewarded density. Collapse each slide
   to: one headline + one focal visual + near-zero body. Kill the hud row + kicker + tag row +
   stat block + pager + button stack as a DEFAULT. Keep at most 1-2 structural-decoration
   moments in the whole deck (this also matches Carter's own "thin not blocky" memory).

2. COMPLETION RATE IS THE HIDDEN CURRENCY. Swipe-through drives 3-5x more non-follower
   distribution. Busy slides slow the swipe and read as drop-offs; poster slides swipe fast and
   clean. Build the deck so slide 1 to slide 2 has near-zero friction, front-load all value into
   slides 1-3, and make every slide a legible standalone entry point (because unseen slides get
   re-served as fresh content).

3. TARGET 7-9 SLIDES for a standard educational deck. Hard floor 5. Only go past 10 when it is a
   genuinely serialized numbered list (a cheat sheet where the numbering itself pulls the swipe).
   Do not pad.

4. COVER = HOOK, NOT TITLE, AND MAKE IT FEEL INCOMPLETE. One idea, 5-8 words, largest element on
   the slide, high-contrast pattern interrupt, and an open loop or partial reveal (cropped
   diagram, blurred result, "#1 of 7", before-with-no-after). Run the standalone stop-the-scroll
   test before shipping. This is a pure poster: one focal point, massive type.

5. BUILD THREE NEW POSTER-NATIVE FORMATS INTO THE PIPELINE:
   a) Seamless panorama: render one wide 3240x1350+ composition, auto-slice into 1080-wide 4:5
      tiles. Best at 3 slides. Never let a face/keyword land on a seam. This is a one-focal-point
      format by construction, so it directly cures busy-ness. Our HTML+shotpls stack can do this
      by rendering a 3240-wide canvas and cropping three viewports.
   b) Frame-by-frame pseudo-animation: render N frames of ONE evolving object (a terminal filling
      in, a diagram building, an attack chain lighting up step by step) as sequential static
      slides. Feels animated, needs no video, 100 percent buildable in the current pipeline. Very
      on-brand for cyber (show a payload/scan/exploit progressing frame by frame).
   c) True video slides (later): a mid-deck or opening MP4 demo slide (real terminal capture,
      60s max, 4:5, H.264) plus native music to tap the Reels algorithm. Highest-ceiling but
      needs a video-capture step outside shotpls; treat as a phase-2 capability.

6. STEAL THE HORMOZI/WELSH TEXT-POSTER CRAFT, NOT THE VISUAL. Distill each slide to the single
   essential phrase (Hormozi does not transcribe, he reduces). Bold heavy type, brutal contrast,
   consistent template across the deck for brand recognition. We keep our technical-brutalist
   identity; we just strip to one message per frame.

7. USE THE HYBRID AESTHETIC AS OUR DIFFERENTIATOR. Designed poster covers + raw real interior
   proof (actual terminal screenshots, real tool output, real job posting screenshots, real CVE
   pages). Authenticity-over-polish is an explicit 2026 tailwind. This lets us out-authority the
   generic Canva-template crowd while staying poster-clean.

8. EXPLOIT THE JUNE 2026 MULTI-CAPTION UPDATE FOR SEO. Give every slide its own caption seeded
   with a different searchable technical term (a distinct tool, cert code, CVE, command per
   slide). Instagram confirmed per-slide captions feed search discovery. This is a free reach
   lever competitors are not yet using. Worth a tiny tooling addition to emit a per-slide caption
   list alongside the slide HTML.

9. SIZE: keep 1080x1350 (4:5) as the render baseline. 3:4 (1080x1440) is officially available
   and gives ~7 percent more height but is NOT the default and NOT mandatory; treat as an
   optional variant, and ignore the "1080x1440 is the new required format" blog hype (unverified).

10. METRIC TO OPTIMIZE: SAVES, then completion rate, then DM sends. Not likes, not comments-count.
    Every design decision should be judged by "does this make the deck more save-worthy and more
    completely-swiped," which is exactly the argument for stripping busy slides down to posters.
