## FINDINGS

### 1. The single most stealable governance move: "master then scale" (Superside)

Source: Superside, "Creating High-Performing Social Media Graphics for Brands In 2026" (superside.com/blog/social-media-graphics). Superside is a large managed creative agency; pricing ~$5,000/mo to $100,000/mo+ (manypixels.co comparison, 2026). Their published production workflow is a four-phase gate, and it is the exact opposite of "spray a hundred variants":

1. GROUNDING. Before any pixels: every asset gets "clear business outcomes, a defined audience, a single message, a proof point and a real offer." One message per asset is a rule, not a suggestion.
2. EXPLORATION. AI tests "a handful of varied directions" (product macro vs benefit-led, lifestyle vs UI imagery), explicitly "rather than generating a hundred minor variants."
3. MASTER CREATION. A human refines ONE strongest concept: "anatomy, composition, type hierarchy, and credibility." That single master "sets the quality bar."
4. SCALE. Only after the master is approved do they generate platform sizes, recompose layouts, localize, and track sources.

Their stated art-direction non-negotiables ("what still decides winners"): "Hierarchy remains the foundation, single focal point, one message per asset." Also: readable type at small sizes, color carrying intent over noise, motion that guides the eye.

Their human/AI split, verbatim: "AI plays the role of a fast junior with perfect memory... The senior role still belongs to your designers and creative leads." Machines handle resizing, background gen, cleanup, adaptive layouts. Humans "decide which concept deserves to exist, tune hierarchy and spacing, choose images that feel made not generic." Stated result: "Productivity without proliferation of lookalike content."

Their brand-system architecture: design tokens (color, type, spacing) + reusable components (cards, carousels, shorts, stories, banners) + regional variants + a connected asset library that links master files to usage rights AND performance data, so "performance is tracked, analyzed and folded back into your templates and prompts."

This is the poster-not-newspaper discipline expressed as process: you win by picking ONE concept and making it excellent, then you industrialize the boring parts.

### 2. Layout taxonomy that varies without sameness (PostNitro "Carousel by Design")

Source: postnitro.ai/blog/post/carousel-by-design. The core insight they name explicitly: "Templates constrain decisions, not visual identity" and layouts "should feel related, not identical." They prescribe 4-5 core layout roles, each with a distinct visual job, reused across every deck:

- Opening / cover: high contrast, bold treatment. Job: hook.
- Early middle: clean hierarchy. Job: define the problem.
- Core middle: varied rhythm. Job: deliver the teaching/proof.
- Late middle: restrained. Job: remove objections.
- Final: natural endpoint. Job: direct action.

The anti-sameness tactics they name, verbatim: vary body-slide rhythm (single-line statements alternating with examples), asymmetric text blocks (different lengths/densities per slide), responsive layout choices within system constraints. And the load-bearing line for Carter's busy-slide problem: "Dense slides CAN work when hierarchy is strong." The problem was never density, it was competing focal points.

Their "production operating system" (5 steps): Create once (reusable slide patterns) -> Store brand once (colors/fonts/logos central) -> Export by channel -> Review in context (mobile readability before approval) -> Archive final + caption for repurposing.

### 3. The art-direction control set and the review checklist (PostNitro)

Source: postnitro.ai/blog/post/art-direction-definition. What an art director locks for a carousel:

- Type scale: "no more than two type styles per slide."
- Image treatment: consistent crop/filter/photographic approach across all slides.
- Contrast: high-contrast headline paired with restrained supporting elements.
- Pacing: "steady text rhythm," a spacing logic that repeats predictably.
- Emotional register: one tone for the deck (sharp / warm / technical / editorial).

Consistency rules: single focal area per slide, repeated compositional logic, one primary accent + neutrals (no style shifts mid-post), coordinated accent devices (do not introduce a new flourish on a random slide).

The five-question review gate (the single most stealable checklist in the whole division):
1. Does the cover make ONE clear visual promise?
2. Do middle slides follow consistent composition logic?
3. Are type, spacing, and color behaving predictably?
4. Does the final slide connect visually to earlier slides?
5. Would the post feel branded WITHOUT the logo?

Q5 is the killer. If you can strip the logo and it still reads as you, your system is real.

PostNitro's second, longer pre-publish checklist (from carousel-by-design): hook quality (does the opening create movement), sequence logic (does each slide make the next feel necessary), copy tightness (one idea per slide, no filler transitions), brand alignment, mobile readability, CTA match, accessibility contrast, correct file export. And the editing test, verbatim: "Remove any line that only sounds intelligent. Keep the lines that make the next action easier."

### 4. Hard proof for "poster not newspaper": Deloitte recall data

Source cited by packagetheworld.com (2026 design guide) and echoed in the 2026 brand-recall search cluster: a Deloitte 2025 study found geometric patterns with 3 or fewer unique elements achieved 71% recall in blind shelf tests, versus 34% for patterns with 6+ elements. It is packaging, not carousels, but the mechanism is identical: peripheral-vision recognition collapses past ~3 elements. Carter's decks currently run 6-9 reading zones per slide. This is the quantitative spine of the entire teardown: fewer elements roughly doubles recall.

### 5. The AI-first agency pipeline (this is Carter's exact model, validated)

Source: Renda, "How Agencies Produce Social Content 5x Faster in 2026" (tryrenda.com/learn/agency-content-workflow). This describes the pipeline Carter already runs, which means his tooling is not fringe, it is the emerging agency standard:

- Strategy/copy: ChatGPT or Claude drafts, human strategist directs and gives final approval.
- Design: Claude Design (Claude Code artifacts) produces original HTML visuals; Canva only for pulling client brand-kit assets.
- Render: HTML exported as Standalone HTML, then converted to per-slide PNGs. Renda is their named tool; Carter's shotpls is the same function.
- Schedule/approve: Buffer / Later / Sprout Social.

Numbers: a carousel that took 3 hours now takes "thirty to forty minutes including revisions." A mid-tier agency ships 40 assets/week. Team shifts from 2.5 FTE designers to 1 designer (the 30% needing real design judgment) plus account managers/strategists doing the AI prompting. Governance move they name: "Set up a separate Claude Design system for each client. Brand colours, fonts, tone of voice, reference carousels."

Render gotchas (Renda, claude-design-formatting): target 1080x1350 for IG 4:5 ("consistently outperforms square for scroll-stopping content"). Every slide ships as a separate 1080x1350 PNG "named in deck order." Naive HTML-to-image tools fail because they do not wait for React hydration, do not neutralize the fit-scaler transform (which leaves "a thin white margin around every slide edge"), and do not hide the carousel chrome. Carter's shotpls already solves the tiling/paint race per his own memory, so this is a confirmation his pipeline is ahead of the generic tools.

### 6. Figma-component agency workflow (for understanding, not adoption)

Source: Usevisuals, "Top 6 Design Workflow Automations Agencies Use to Scale in 2026" (usevisuals.com/blog/top-design-workflow-automations-agencies). The non-AI agency stack: Figma + Zapier + Make.com + Airtable. Flow: non-designers type copy/images into Airtable or a Google Sheet -> triggers a Figma template populate (Auto Layout absorbs text-length variation so nothing breaks) -> multi-format export (PNG/PDF/SVG) at once. Review gate is automated: Make.com watches Figma for status changes, generates low-res previews, posts them to Slack with approve/revise buttons, then auto-exports approved files to Drive. Claimed 70%+ production-time cut, "50+ social posts from a content calendar" per workflow run.

Core principle they state: treat "designs as software components rather than static art," where a single color-variable change cascades across every template. This is the same idea as Carter's per-slide HTML files sharing a token layer, just in a GUI tool. The stealable bit is the SLACK APPROVAL GATE with low-res previews, not the Figma stack itself.

### 7. Slide-role briefing = tighter AI prompts (the parallel-generation insight)

Source: the creative-brief search cluster, agency-carousel framing (2026). Key line: "Strategy can write one brief with six card jobs, and design can build one component set. AI-assisted image generation can follow tighter prompts because each frame has a defined purpose instead of vague instructions like 'make this one more benefit-led.'" And: "carousel uniformity saves time, as mixed card ratios create avoidable QA issues, especially when different designers or AI tools generate assets in parallel." Also the master-concept move restated: "build one master concept, create export presets for portrait and square, and treat every other ratio as a derivative."

For a one-man op fanning out slides to parallel AI generation, this is the unlock: give each slide a named role in the brief BEFORE generating, so each HTML file gets a purpose-specific prompt instead of a vague one. Defined-purpose frames = better AI output and no QA drift.

### 8. Video-slide-inside-carousel, systematized (directly answers the animated-slide competitor gap)

Sources: postnitro.ai/blog/post/video-in-carousel, and the 2026 IG carousel algorithm cluster (trymypost.com, creatorflow.so, socialhabitmarketing.com; Metricool cited Feb 2026).

Engagement data (PostNitro table): image+video carousel 2.33% avg engagement, image-only 1.74%, video-only 1.45%. Carousels overall average the highest engagement of any format in 2026 (~1.92% cited in the truefuture/trymypost cluster).

Placement rules (three sanctioned patterns):
- Video on slide 1: instant motion as a pattern interrupt for pure attention/scroll-stop.
- Video in slides 3-7: use the first slides to set up a problem, then the video "swoops in as the solution / demo / testimonial" for credibility.
- Video on final slide: personal CTA close.

Hard specs: MP4 only, max 60s per video slide, 4GB file cap, up to 20 slides now (was 10 before 2024). CRITICAL governance rule (Metricool, Feb 2026): "All images and videos in a single carousel should use the SAME aspect ratio." Mixing portrait/square/landscape "creates jarring transitions and reduces engagement." So the whole deck stays 4:5. Also: burn in captions, assume sound-off. Music on a carousel pushes it into the Reels feed for extra reach.

Structural pattern they recommend: static hook -> video demo in the middle -> static CTA close. Motion for pacing, not decoration.

## SO WHAT FOR US

Carter's own 90-day data already proved the thesis (image-artifact decks 47-65k views / 2,700-6,600 saves vs text-template decks 10-18k views / 100-570 saves). This division supplies the PROCESS to make every deck hit the winner pattern on purpose, not by luck.

1. ADOPT "master then scale" as the deck workflow (Superside). Do not design 8 slides in parallel from a blank brief. Design ONE hero slide (usually the cover) to completion first, lock its type scale, accent, and single focal point, and let it set the bar. Then generate the rest as derivatives of that master. This is a one-line change to the make-carousel skill: a "build and approve the cover master before generating body slides" gate.

2. BUILD A 4-5 LAYOUT-ROLE SYSTEM, not one template (PostNitro). Define named roles: cover (hook), problem, teaching/proof, objection/turn, CTA. Each role is a distinct HTML layout with its OWN focal-point rule, sharing the token layer. "Related, not identical." This directly kills the newspaper problem: each slide gets ONE job and ONE focal point by construction. Encode the roles into the skill so the LLM must assign every slide a role before writing its HTML.

3. SHIP THE 3-ELEMENT RULE as a hard constraint (Deloitte 71% vs 34%). Cap every slide at 3 reading zones maximum. This is the single most important, most measurable change. Add it to the render/review checklist as a blocker: if a slide has more than 3 distinct zones, it fails. This alone converts newspapers into posters.

4. INSTALL THE 5-QUESTION REVIEW GATE (PostNitro) as an automated post-render pass. After shotpls renders the PNGs, run a vision check: (a) one clear visual promise on cover, (b) consistent composition logic across middles, (c) predictable type/spacing/color, (d) final connects to earlier, (e) reads as branded with the logo removed. Q5 (logo-off recognizability) is the north star for building a recognizable series that drives follows. This is buildable now as a Claude vision pass over the rendered 2160x2700 PNGs.

5. NAME SLIDE ROLES IN THE BRIEF BEFORE GENERATING (creative-brief cluster). When fanning slides out to parallel generation, give each its purpose first. Defined-purpose frames produce tighter prompts and zero QA drift. Fits Carter's terminal-orchestration model perfectly: the orchestrator writes a 6-8 card brief with a role per card, then dispatches one generator per card.

6. ADD ANIMATED VIDEO SLIDES, systematized (PostNitro/Metricool). This is the competitor gap Carter flagged. Rules to bake in: keep the whole deck 4:5 (never mix aspect ratios), MP4 max 60s per slide, burn captions, motion for pacing not decoration. Sanctioned placements: motion cover for scroll-stop, OR motion in slides 3-7 as the "solution reveal," OR motion CTA close. Data justification: image+video 2.33% vs image-only 1.74%. Practically, this means extending the pipeline to render one or two slides as short MP4s (e.g., an HTML+CSS-animation slide captured to video, or a Remotion/ffmpeg step) while the rest stay shotpls PNGs. Same 1080x1350 canvas throughout.

7. PERFORMANCE FEEDS THE TEMPLATES (Superside). Carter already tracks per-deck saves/views. Formalize it: tag each shipped deck with its layout roles and focal-point choices, then fold the winners back into the role library. The system should get better every cycle. This is the connected-asset-library idea for a solo op: a simple JSON log linking deck -> roles used -> saves.

8. THE PIPELINE IS ALREADY RIGHT (Renda validation). The Claude-Code-artifact-to-HTML-to-PNG pipeline Carter runs is the exact emerging 2026 agency standard, and shotpls already solves the render gotchas (hydration wait, fit-scaler margin, chrome-hiding, tiling race) that break naive tools. Do not rebuild the pipeline. Invest the effort in the GOVERNANCE LAYER on top of it: roles, the 3-element cap, the master-first gate, and the 5-question vision review. That is where studios win, and it is exactly what a one-man AI-leveraged op can automate end to end.

Bottom line: studios do not out-design Carter slide by slide. They win with a system: one master, a small set of named layout roles, a hard element cap, and a fixed review gate, with AI industrializing the boring parts. Every one of those moves is code-able into the make-carousel skill and a post-render vision pass. Build the governance layer, keep the pipeline.
