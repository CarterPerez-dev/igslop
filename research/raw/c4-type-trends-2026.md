## FINDINGS

### 1. The macro shift: uniformity out, character in. Serifs and grotesques are the story, geometric sans is dying.

Consistent across every 2026 source. After a decade of stripped-back geometric sans dominance, 2026 type is about personality, craft, and "joy." Creative Bloq (2026) frames it verbatim: "uniformity is out, character is in." Envato and Fontfabric echo this.

The most concrete death list comes from Typewolf's 2026 usage data (3,000+ sites tracked, plus Fonts In Use, Awwwards, and pro portfolios), reported via madegooddesigns.com/popular-fonts:
- Proxima Nova now "signals 2012 to 2018 web design."
- Montserrat and Poppins are "associated with free templates and budget projects due to extreme overuse" ("Poppins fatigue," per font-trends-2026).
- Inter has replaced Open Sans as the default interface sans.
- Neo-grotesques (Sohne, GT America, Founders Grotesk) have displaced geometric sans as the "serious" premium choice. Sohne is called out as the Stripe / Linear / Y Combinator startup default.

DIRECT IMPLICATION: if Carter's decks lean on a geometric sans (Poppins/Montserrat family), that alone reads "budget template" to design-literate viewers in 2026. Moving to a grotesque or a display serif is the single highest-signal upgrade.

### 3. CONDENSED GROTESQUES (the poster/brutalist headline workhorse) — free and named

Exact bucket for a technical-brutalist deck. All free on Google Fonts unless noted:

- Bebas Neue (free, Google Fonts). "One of the most popular free display fonts in the world." Tall, condensed, all-caps, four weights (Thin to Bold), tight spacing and vertical emphasis. Best for maximum headline size in constrained layouts.
- Anton (free, Google Fonts). Single weight. "Bolder and more condensed than Oswald, with a tighter overall rhythm, punchy and urgent." Listed under the Brutalist/Industrial Display movement.
- Oswald (free, Google Fonts). Six weights. Condensed but less extreme than Bebas. Oswald was based directly on Alternate Gothic (Morris Fuller Benton).
- Archivo Black (free, Google Fonts). Brutalist display, heavy.
- Bowlby One (free, Google Fonts). Brutalist display.
- Barlow Condensed (free, Google Fonts). "California-inspired grotesk," space-efficient, condensed proportions maximize headline size.
- League Gothic and Roboto Condensed (free). Named Oswald/Bebas alternatives with near-identical condensed proportions.

Paid tier these free faces imitate (the "real" brutalist canon): Druk and Druk Wide (Commercial Type, Berton Hasebe) is the defining paid brutalist/condensed display, "unapologetically massive," heavy weights for editorial/poster/music. Also Knockout, Compressa, Trade Gothic Bold Condensed, PP Neue Machina, F37 Wicker, National Park, Monument Extended (Pangram Pangram, brutalist sans). Anton and Archivo Black are the closest free stand-ins for Druk energy.

### 4. WIDE / LUXURY ALL-CAPS DISPLAY and SLAB REVIVAL

Wide display is a distinct 2026 movement (trend 6, "Luxury All-Caps Wide Display"), the opposite pole from condensed. Wide, airy, all-caps display signals premium/luxury.
- Free wide/all-caps display: Italiana, Cinzel, Forum (all Google Fonts, serif display, all-caps luxury).
- Paid wide: Sohne Breit (Klim), Tobias, Reckless Neue, Monument Extended (Pangram Pangram).

SLAB SERIF REVIVAL (background-to-primary in 2026): the "optical slab" with humanist proportions and warm on-screen texture is returning. Named example: Jubilat by Josh Darden (Darden Studio, paid), six weights, "generous curves, efficient spacing, made for headlines, decks, and sidebars." Broader read from Creative Bloq: "chunky, juicy serifs" are a major 2026 trend, already appearing in corporate branding, plus a "Mutant Heritage" idea, classic letterforms and mid-century grotesks reengineered "off-kilter, tech-tuned yet visibly handcrafted."

### 6. MONO ACCENTS — "Retro Monospaced Revival" (trend 4), high-signal for a cyber brand

Strong, recent, directly on-brand for cybersecurity. Mono in 2026 is NOT just a dev tool: used for magazine covers, indie product branding, editorial layouts, design-system docs, and the "mono everywhere" aesthetic that "gives a design technical credibility and a hint of analog computing nostalgia." Aesthetic borrows from terminal interfaces, typewriter prints, architectural drafting.

CRITICAL usage rule from the source: mono body text over multiple columns is "exhausting to read." The win is RESTRAINT, mono as an ACCENT typeface (labels, kickers, stats, tags, captions, code-strings) creates "a distinctive rhythm" and "technical credibility" against proportional body text.

Named mono faces (free unless noted):
- JetBrains Mono (free). Named for editorial/product branding.

- Geist Mono (free). Bouba-companion mono.

### 7. VARIABLE FONTS AND OPTICAL-SIZE TRICKS — real axes, real CSS, free faces

Variable fonts have "crossed from trend to best practice" in 2026 (multiple sources). Two payoffs: one file (100-200KB) replaces four static files (400-800KB), and axes are treated as design tokens. Tricks that matter for poster/social type:

OPTICAL SIZE (opsz) is the headline trick. It automatically retunes contrast, x-height, spacing, and character width for the set size. Large headlines go thinner and more elegant; small text goes thicker and more readable. Two free faces ship a true opsz axis and fit a deck perfectly:

- Fraunces (free, variable). Four axes: wght (weight), opsz (Optical Size, ships 9pt / 72pt / 144pt masters), SOFT (softness/inkiness 0 to 100), WONK (0/1, swaps in "wonky" glyphs, auto at small sizes but forceable ON at display size for character). As opsz DECREASES the x-height rises, spacing opens, characters widen. For a big carousel headline you set a HIGH opsz (72 to 144) to get the elegant high-contrast display cut, and can flip WONK on for a signature look.
  CSS: `font-variation-settings: 'opsz' 144, 'wght' 600, 'SOFT' 0, 'WONK' 1;`
  Source: https://github.com/undercasetype/Fraunces , https://fraunces.undercase.xyz/ , https://www.beautifulwebtype.com/fraunces/

- Bricolage Grotesque (free, variable, Google Fonts). Axes: weight, width, optical size, grade (GRAD). By Mathieu Triay (a fork of Mayenne Sans). Shapes get "tighter and more detailed at large sizes, more open at small." Display weights are "dramatic and expressive." A free grotesque giving brutalist-adjacent display AND a clean text cut from one file.
  Source: https://github.com/ateliertriay/bricolage , https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Bricolage+Grotesque , https://pimpmytype.com/font/bricolage-grotesque/

- Google Fonts documents opsz as a first-class axis: https://fonts.google.com/knowledge/glossary/optical_size_axis

GRADE (GRAD) axis is called out as the 2026 standard for UI faces: changes apparent weight WITHOUT changing width, so you can darken text on hover/emphasis with ZERO layout shift.

ANIMATION (relevant even for static decks if a slide is rendered to video):
- `font-variation-settings` is fully animatable: CSS transitions it on hover, keyframes it, or drives it from a scroll timeline via `animation-timeline` with NO JavaScript, because CSS Fonts Level 4 defines continuous interpolation between axis values (wght 300 to 700 produces every value between).
- Named technique (Carmen Ansio, carmenansio.com/articles/variable-font-scroll): sticky element in a 300vh wrapper, `animation-range: contain 0% contain 100%` locks the weight sweep to the pinned duration.
- PERFORMANCE CAVEAT (every technical source): animating wght / font-variation-settings triggers text relayout, so animate ONLY large display text (hero headlines), never body. Maps exactly to a per-slide video: one big animated headline, nothing else moving.

Sources: https://www.carmenansio.com/articles/variable-font-scroll , https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Properties/font-variation-settings , https://www.kittl.com/blogs/why-variable-fonts-are-winning-fnt/ , https://medium.com/design-bootcamp/typography-trends-2026-2027-when-letters-begin-to-breathe-8499fb6c5ef1

### 9. PROVEN PAIRING SYSTEMS (named, most are free)

The 2026 formula (font-pairing sources): ONE sans "workhorse" body + ONE "speaker" headline. Reliable patterns: modern sans body + neo-grotesk headline; modern sans body + high-contrast display serif; modern sans body + geometric display sans. Pair by contrast of weight/width/x-height FIRST, only then change family. Pairings fail on rhythm (leading + letter-spacing) more than on font choice.

Free superfamilies (built-in harmony, zero pairing risk):
- DM Sans + DM Serif Display + DM Mono (Colophon, the DeepMind family, all free). A ready-made sans + display-serif + mono system at no cost.
- IBM Plex Sans + Plex Serif + Plex Mono (free).
- Source Sans + Source Serif + Source Code (free).
- Noto / PT families (free).

Named 2026 pairings that fit a technical brand:
- Fraunces (display serif headline) + Fragment Mono or JetBrains Mono (accent/body). Called THE indie-editorial signature of 2026.
- Space Grotesk (headline) + DM Serif Display, "tension between tradition and experimentation, modern and intentional."
- Epilogue (humanist grotesque) + Fraunces, "literary without being pretentious."
- Space Mono + Plus Jakarta Sans, "retro-tech + friendly-professional."

The cybersecurity-optimal system, assembled from the above (all free): a heavy condensed grotesque or Fraunces-display headline + a clean grotesque body (Space Grotesk / Hanken Grotesk) + a mono accent (Space Mono / IBM Plex Mono / JetBrains Mono) for kickers, stats, tags, and code-strings. Three faces max: "two feel intentional, three can work if one is subtle, four looks chaotic" (Instagram-carousel sources).

### 10. WHAT CAROUSEL/SOCIAL DESIGNERS SPECIFICALLY DO WITH TYPE (poster read, not newspaper)

Directly answers the "poster not newspaper" problem:
- "Bold typography cuts through digital noise." Bold headline fonts, SHORT words, high contrast. "If you can't read the headline in 2 to 3 seconds, simplify: fewer words, bigger text, calmer font, more spacing."
- Sizing ratio cited: title ~4.0em, sub-title ~3.0em, body ~1.5em. A roughly 2.5x jump from body to title, far steeper hierarchy than stacking many equal-weight reading zones.
- Font count: two feel intentional, three max, four is chaos.
- "Poster" (the typeface, expressive serifs) named as a headline face that makes text the focal point.
- Motion (2026 carousel/reel trend, relevant since shotpls can render a slide to video): kinetic typography is now "a major part of motion graphic design, advertising, UI, and brand identity," not decoration. Even "a pulsing headline or a sliding text reveal significantly improves engagement." The "hold and scroll" / swipe-native carousel is a 2026 format where the algorithm rewards swipes; type-forward single-focal slides drive that.

## SO WHAT FOR US

1. Kill any geometric-sans default. If the decks lean Poppins/Montserrat/generic-geometric, that reads "free template" to design-literate 2026 eyes (Typewolf usage data). Switch the headline system to a grotesque or a variable display serif. Highest-leverage single move.

2. Adopt a 3-face free system, mono-accented, on-brand for cyber. All free, all Google Fonts / open license so they base64-embed cleanly in the shotpls pipeline:
   - HEADLINE: pick per deck. Brutalist/urgent: Anton or Archivo Black (free Druk stand-in) or Bebas Neue condensed. Editorial/authority: Fraunces at high opsz (72 to 144) with WONK on for signature, or Bricolage Grotesque display weights.
   - BODY / SUB: Space Grotesk or Hanken Grotesk (grotesque, not geometric).
   - ACCENT (the cyber tell): a mono, Space Mono or IBM Plex Mono or JetBrains Mono, ONLY for kickers, stats, tags, code-strings, pager. Mono-as-accent buys "technical credibility" for free and is a named 2026 trend, not a gimmick.

3. Exploit optical size to fix the "busy" problem. The current decks stack 6-9 equal reading zones. opsz lets ONE massive headline carry the slide: set Fraunces/Bricolage at high opsz so the big cut goes thin/elegant and high-contrast, then strip the slide to that one focal line plus a small mono kicker. opsz does the hierarchy work stacked rows currently do. Target the cited 2.5x title-to-body ratio (title ~4em vs body ~1.5em).

4. Variable fonts are a build-tooling win, not just aesthetics. One variable file (Fraunces, Bricolage) replaces multiple static weights, smaller base64 payload per HTML slide, and unlocks per-slide axis tuning (heavier headline on the hook slide, thinner on interiors) from a SINGLE embedded font. Use GRAD for emphasis states with zero reflow if any slide animates.

5. Animated slides are now table stakes and the tooling already fits. shotpls renders HTML; a variable-font weight/opsz sweep is pure CSS (`font-variation-settings` + `@keyframes` or scroll-timeline, no JS). The proven pattern: animate ONLY the one big headline (relayout is cheap on one line, catastrophic on body), matching the "one focal point per slide" poster goal. Build a shotpls-to-video path for slide 1 (the hook) first: a headline that sweeps wght 300 to 900 or opsz 9 to 144 is low-effort, high-signal, 2026-current.

6. Three faces max, short words, 2-to-3-second readability. Enforce as a deck lint rule: if a slide can't be read in 2 to 3 seconds, too many words or too small a headline. The concrete fix for "reads like a designed newspaper."

7. Signature move to own the format: Fraunces WONK + a mono kicker. A high-opsz Fraunces headline with WONK glyphs ON, over a single Space Mono / IBM Plex Mono kicker, is a specific, free, 2026-current, on-brand-for-cyber look almost no competitor is running. A concrete route to "best carousels on Instagram."
