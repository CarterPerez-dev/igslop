## FINDINGS

### TIER 1: the closest models to what Carter should become (minimal, poster, concept-per-frame)

**1. Visualize Value / Jack Butcher (@visualizevalue)**
- Followers: 482K, 1,099 posts (search snippet, 2026). Personal @jckbtchr = 23K.
- This is the single most on-target reference for a poster aesthetic. Started Jan 2019 as a daily practice: one black-and-white image distilling one idea about leverage, value, or independent thinking. About 1,000 pieces published across IG and Twitter. Background: a decade in agencies (Ferrari, Showtime, Godiva) before this. Work sold at Christie's, Art Basel, Sotheby's; NFT projects generated 1B+ in ETH sales since 2021.
- Slide anatomy: strict two-color system, black on white (or inverted). One concept per frame reduced to a diagram. Line-weight iconography, monospace-adjacent geometric sans, heavy negative space, everything centered on one idea. The recurring visual grammar is a fixed toolkit of ~12 "visual devices" he reuses: scale, comparison, metaphor, pattern recognition, flow, stacking, continuum, Venn, hierarchy, Cartesian plot, loops, spectra (source: designdisciplin.com "How to Design Like Visualize Value" masterclass breakdown). The lesson is that the SYSTEM is a small fixed vocabulary of diagram types applied to endless topics, so every post is instantly recognizable as his.
- Words per frame: near zero to a single short caption. The image is the argument.
- So what: this is the proof that ruthless two-color discipline plus a fixed diagram vocabulary beats decoration. His frames are essentially single-slide "carousels" (one focal point), which is exactly the direction Carter needs.
- Source: instagram.com/visualizevalue, designdisciplin.com/p/how-to-design-like-vv, visualizevalue.com/about

**2. Studio Feixen (@studiofeixen)** [Swiss design studio, Lucerne; Felix Pfaffli]
- Followers: 214K (search snippet, 2026).
- The reference for ANIMATED slides done at the highest craft level. Known for "looping, satisfyingly designed, glorious colour" moving digital posters as a side project. They celebrated follower milestones with custom animated posters (started at 30,000 followers end of 2016). Clients: Nike, Google, NYT.
- Technique (from itsnicethat.com feature): moving posters built on responsive-programming ideas, "dynamic type," wobbling numbers, type that stretches its own content, objects (computers, tablets, iPhones) changing scale. Loops are seamless and hypnotic. Bold flat color fields, one dominant type object per frame. Pfaffli frames it as "What will the future poster look like?"
- So what: this is the ceiling for the animated-slide format. A carousel where slide 1 (or a mid-slide) is a seamless looping type animation, flat bold color, single focal element, is the Feixen move. Directly informs the video-slide build Carter wants. Study these loops for the "satisfying" quality: single element, single motion, perfect loop point, no clutter.
- Source: instagram.com/studiofeixen, itsnicethat.com/articles/studio-feixen-posters-graphic-design-230118, studiofeixen.ch

**3. The Futur / Chris Do (@thefutur, @thechrisdo)**
- Followers: 800K+ organically (their own video title "How I Got 800K+ Followers on Instagram Organically"). Confirmed by multiple search snippets.
- Not minimal per se, but the most explicit PUBLISHED methodology for sequential carousel design, and they sell a template kit. Chris Do teaches "sequential design," how to lay slides so messaging and visuals connect. Their stated craft rules (from The Futur typography material referenced across results):

  - Explicit anti-pattern they call out: "poorly designed layouts that use too many visual gimmicks, colors, or that suffer from low contrast impede the audience's ability to read." This is a direct diagnosis of Carter's current "busy" problem.

  - "The key to engaging carousels is the writing," then color palette + font pairings + consistent photo/illustration style for instant brand recognition.
- So what: the pull-quote for Carter is that gimmicks, too many colors, and low contrast are the enemy. Their light/dark alternating-post idea is a cheap feed-level upgrade. Their kit proves the market rewards a locked, recognizable system over per-post novelty.

### TECHNIQUE FINDINGS (numbers, 2026, all attributed)

Slide anatomy consensus across current guides:
- One idea per slide. Under 30 words per slide is the repeated rule; some say 40-50 max; slide 1 under ~40 characters so no zoom needed. (creatorflow.so, wavegen.ai, marketersclique.com, trymypost.com)
- Type massive: 60-100pt on a 1080-wide canvas; minimum body ~40px. Headline the largest object; one headline + one subhead max; avoid paragraphs. (colorkuler.com, futuristicmarketingservices.com, marketersclique.com)
- Consistency is the system: same type family, same palette, recurring graphic elements across all slides so it reads as one deck. (multiple)
- Slide count: 7-10 is the engagement sweet spot for text/graphic decks; 8-12 for educational. Engagement dips after slide 3 and recovers at slide 8+. (creatorflow.so, trymypost.com, socialhabitmarketing.com)

Format / dimensions (2026):
- 1080x1350 (4:5) is the default. NEW in 2026: 1080x1440 (3:4) takes even more feed space and fills the new 3:4 profile grid without cropping. (contentdrips.com, wavegen.ai, trymypost.com) Carter renders 1080x1350 today; the 3:4 is worth testing.
- Carousels now support up to 20 mixed images and videos; all slides must share one aspect ratio. (creatorflow.so, trymypost.com)

Animated / video-slide technique (the trend Carter is chasing), with hard numbers:
- Mixed-media carousels (images + video) get 2.33% engagement vs 1.80% image-only. Highest-engagement carousel format in 2026. (multiple; reelbase.io, marketingagent.blog)
- Adding subtle motion to slide 1 (cinemagraph or subtle zoom) increases stop-scroll power by up to 22% vs static headers. (trymypost.com, marketingagent.blog)
- Video placement strategy: video on slide 1 = instant motion pattern-interrupt; OR video in slides 3-7 as the "payoff" after a static setup. Return to static for CTA. (postnitro.ai, creatorflow.so)
- Even one 5-second mid-carousel clip meaningfully increases dwell time. Video slides can be up to 60s. (creatorflow.so)
- Instagram carousels overall: 1.92% avg engagement vs 0.50% Reels vs 0.45% static (2026 benchmark); algorithm re-serves carousels to non-swipers; DM shares weighted 3-5x over likes. (carouselli.com, truefuturemedia.com, trymypost.com)

Seamless / panoramic carousel technique (a specific poster move):
- One wide image split across slides so it flows uninterrupted on swipe. Sweet spot is 3 slides (keeps ~80% swipe-completion). For a 3-slide panorama use a landscape source at least 3240px wide; each slide 1080x1350 or 1080x1080. (postplanify.com, 7labs.io, usevisuals.com)
- Constraint: all slides must be STILL for a true panorama; a mid video slide breaks the continuous effect. Keep faces/text off the split lines. So panorama and video-slide are mutually exclusive within one post. (postplanify.com)
- Panoramas earn ~1.4x more reach than single images. (postplanify.com)

Cover-slide formula (repeated across sources): bold hook headline 5-8 words as the largest object + a visual pattern-interrupt (high-contrast color, expressive face, or dramatic graphic) + a curiosity trigger (partial reveal, surprising stat, open question). Slide 1 carries ~60-80% of swipe-through weight; audiences give ~0.25-0.7s. (resont.com, socialhabitmarketing.com, 2slides.com)

## SO WHAT FOR US

1. The direct model is Visualize Value, not the marketing carousel farms. Two-color discipline plus a small FIXED vocabulary of diagram/composition types applied to endless cybersecurity topics is the whole game. Carter should define a locked set of maybe 8-12 slide archetypes (hero number, one-line verdict, single diagram, giant word, tool card, etc.) and reuse them forever. Recognizability comes from the system, not per-post novelty. This is the opposite of stacking 6-9 reading zones per slide.

2. Kill the newspaper. The Futur states the anti-pattern outright: too many gimmicks, too many colors, low contrast. Carter's current slide (hud row + kicker + multi-line headline + rule + paragraph + tag row + stat block + pager + button) is exactly that. Target one focal point per slide, under 30 words, type at 60-100pt, and delete at least half the reading zones.

3. Animated slides are real and measurable, not a gimmick. A cinemagraph/looping type slide 1 is worth up to +22% stop-scroll; mixed media pulls 2.33% vs 1.80%. The craft ceiling is Studio Feixen: one element, one motion, a perfect seamless loop, flat bold color. This is buildable in Carter's terminal pipeline: render a short HTML/CSS or canvas animation and capture it to an MP4 slide with a headless-Chrome frame-capture step (extend shotpls to grab N frames, stitch with ffmpeg). One looping hero slide per carousel, static for the rest, is the highest-leverage first build.

4. The cover slide is the brand. Every account cited for strength was cited for a recognizable cover. Lock ONE cover archetype for @carterperez.dev (fixed grid, fixed type object, fixed color signature) so the feed reads as one body of work. Feed-level contrast trick from The Futur: alternate light/dark covers between posts.

5. There is a wide-open lane. No cybersecurity account found (including the 2M @cybersecuritygirl) is doing poster-minimal carousels. The design-quality bar in the niche is low. Bringing Visualize-Value-grade discipline to cyber content is a genuine differentiator and makes "best carousels on Instagram" plausible within the niche.

6. Test the 3:4 (1080x1440) format. New in 2026, more feed real estate, fills the new profile grid uncropped. Cheap experiment against the current 1080x1350.

7. Panorama is a separate weapon for the right topic (a wide attack-chain diagram, a timeline, a kill-chain). Use 3-slide panoramas for those, keep them all-static, and reserve animated slides for non-panorama decks since the two are mutually exclusive.
